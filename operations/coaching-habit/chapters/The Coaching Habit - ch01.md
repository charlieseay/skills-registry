# 1. The Kickstart Question — "What's on your mind?"

## What it solves

Managers often don't coach because they don't know how to *start*. Three failure patterns show up instead:

- **The Small Talk Tango** — warming up with trivia and never bridging to what matters
- **The Ossified Agenda** — the same standing-meeting agenda, dead on arrival
- **The Default Diagnosis** — assuming you already know the topic and jumping straight in

## The question

**"What's on your mind?"** — a Goldilocks question: open enough to invite the real topic, focused enough not to be an invitation to ramble about everything. It says: *let's talk about the thing that matters most*, and hands the person the autonomy to choose it rather than you telling them.

## Two types of coaching this sets up

- **Coaching for performance** — fixing/addressing a specific problem. Everyday, necessary, common.
- **Coaching for development** — shifting focus from the issue to the person managing the issue. Rarer, more powerful. Ask "for you" at the end of questions to nudge toward this.

## The 3P Model (use after the Kickstart Question to add focus)

Once they've named what's on their mind, offer three facets:

- **Project** — the content of the situation, the actual work
- **People** — the relationship, and specifically your role in it
- **Patterns** — recurring behaviors or ways of working they'd like to change

*"So there are three different facets we could look at: the project side, the people side, and patterns — if there's a way you're getting in your own way. Where should we start?"*

## Build your new habit

- **WHEN THIS HAPPENS...** A direct report drops by, a customer calls, someone sits down at lunch, a 1:1 starts, an email/IM arrives — any conversation opening.
- **INSTEAD OF...** Small talk, moving straight to advice-giving, defaulting to the standard agenda, or telling the person the topic.
- **I WILL...** Ask "What's on your mind?" (If the trigger is written — email/IM — you can send this as the reply.)
