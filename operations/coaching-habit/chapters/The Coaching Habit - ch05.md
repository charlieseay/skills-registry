# 5. The Lazy Question — "How can I help?"

## The trap of being "helpful"

Edgar Schein's insight (from *Helping*): offering help "one-ups" you and lowers the other person's status, even with the best intentions. Recipients often feel resistance, frustration, and disempowerment — not gratitude.

## The Karpman Drama Triangle

A practical model (built on Transactional Analysis) for three dysfunctional roles people cycle through, often within a single conversation:

| Role | Core belief | Dynamic | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Victim** | "My life is so hard, poor me." | "It's not my fault (it's theirs)." | No sense of agency; known as ineffective; attracts Rescuers |
| **Persecutor** | "I'm surrounded by fools." | "It's not my fault (it's yours)." | Ends up responsible for everything; creates Victims; known as a micromanager |
| **Rescuer** | "Let me jump in and fix it." | "It's my fault/responsibility (not yours)." | People reject the help; creates Victims; perpetuates the triangle |

Most people's default role: **Rescuer.** No one is inherently one role — they're behaviors triggered in the moment, and everyone cycles through all three.

## The question

**"How can I help?"** does two things: (1) forces the other person to make a direct, clear request — they may not have known what they actually wanted until asked; (2) stops *you* from assuming you know how to help and leaping into Rescuer mode.

**Blunter variant:** "What do you want from me?" — strips it to the essential exchange. Land it carefully: it can read as aggressive (Persecutor mode), whiny (Victim mode), or smothering (Rescuer mode) depending on your own state. Soften with "Out of curiosity..." or "Just so I know...".

## Once you get an answer, you still have options

Getting a request doesn't obligate a Yes:
- **"Yes."**
- **"No, I can't do that."**
- **"I can't do that, but I could do [counter-offer]."**
- **"Let me think about that."** (buys time)

## Resisting direct appeals for advice ("How do I...?")

Instead of answering:
> "That's a great question. I've got some ideas, which I'll share with you. But before I do, what are your first thoughts?"

Then, after they answer: "That's terrific. What else could you do?" — chain the AWE Question until they've run out of ideas, *then* offer your own idea if it's still needed.

## Build your new habit

- **WHEN THIS HAPPENS...** Someone asks "How do I...?" or "Could you...?"; the perfect solution pops into your head; you think "it's faster to just do this myself."
- **INSTEAD OF...** Jumping into helpful/action mode; giving the solution; assuming you know the request before it's been made clear.
- **I WILL...** Ask, "How can I help?" (or, more directly, "What do you want from me?")
