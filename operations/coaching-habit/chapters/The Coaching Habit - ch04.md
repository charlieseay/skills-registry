# 4. The Foundation Question — "What do you want?"

## Why it's hard to answer (and ask)

Peter Block's definition of an adult-to-adult relationship: being able to ask for what you want, knowing the answer might be No. Also called "the Goldfish Question" for the response it provokes — eyes widening, mouth opening with nothing coming out. Reasons it's hard:

- We often genuinely don't know what we want until pushed past the first, fast answer.
- Even knowing, it's hard to ask (bad timing, fear of No, "who am I to ask").
- Even asking, it's hard to say clearly (hidden under rhetoric, hedged, assumed-obvious).
- Even heard, it's hard to accept an answer that isn't Yes.

## Wants vs. needs (Marshall Rosenberg / Nonviolent Communication)

- **Want** — the surface, tactical request ("I'd like to have this").
- **Need** — the deeper human driver behind it ("I must have this"). Nine universal needs (Manfred Max-Neef): **Affection, Creation, Recreation, Freedom, Identity, Understanding, Participation, Protection, Subsistence.**

Example: "I want you to talk to the VP for me" might really be about *protection* (I'm too junior) or *participation* (I need you to do your part). Listen past the want for the need.

## Ask a question, trade an answer

Asking "What do you want?" and *also answering it yourself* amplifies the question's power — it's a reciprocal exchange, not an interrogation.

## The neuroscience: TERA Quotient

The brain scans for safety ~5x/second. Four drivers of whether a moment reads as safe (reward) or dangerous (fight-or-flight):

- **T — Tribe:** "Are you with me or against me?"
- **E — Expectation:** "Do I know what happens next?"
- **R — Rank:** "Am I more or less important right now?" (situational, not title-based)
- **A — Autonomy:** "Do I get a say?"

Asking "What do you want?" raises Tribe (you're helping them solve it, not dictating), Rank (you let them go first), and Autonomy (you assume they can generate answers).

## Build your new habit

- **WHEN THIS HAPPENS...** A conversation feels stuck or circling; someone's procrastinating and you're not sure why; a conversation with anyone (report, boss, customer, vendor) has gone off the rails.
- **INSTEAD OF...** Assuming you (or they) already know what they want; imposing your own idea/opinion/course of action; staying stuck without naming it.
- **I WILL...** Ask, "What do you want?" (Bonus: answer it for yourself too.)
