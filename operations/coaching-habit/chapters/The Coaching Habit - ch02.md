# 2. The AWE Question — "And what else?"

Called "the Best Coaching Question in the World" in the book. Three words, three mechanisms.

## Why it works

1. **More options → better decisions.** Paul Nutt's research on 168 organizational decisions found 71% were binary (do this / don't do this), with a >50% failure rate. Adding just one more option before deciding cut the failure rate to ~30%.
2. **It reins in your own Advice Monster.** Every time you ask "And what else?" instead of offering your idea, you stay in curiosity mode instead of solving mode.
3. **It buys you thinking time** when you're not sure what's going on yet.

## How to use it well

- **Stay curious, stay genuine** — don't let it become a bored, rote prompt.
- **Ask it at least 3 times, rarely more than 5.** People ask this question too *few* times, not too many.
- **Recognize success** — when they say "there is nothing else," that's the goal, not a failure. Take a breath and move to the next question.
- **Move on when energy drops** — "Is there anything else?" is the wrap-up variant; it invites closure while leaving the door open.

## The paradox of choice — don't overdo it

More options are good, but not unlimited options. A grocery-store jam study: 24 flavors drew more browsers but 10x fewer purchases than a 6-flavor table — decision paralysis. Cognitive research suggests ~4 is the ideal chunk size for options. Goal: 3-5 solid answers, not a "bazillion."

## Where to drop it in

- After "What's on your mind?" → "And what else?"
- After someone describes a planned action → "And what else could you do?"
- After a timid first answer to the Focus Question → "And what else is a challenge here for you?"
- During brainstorming, to keep energy up without getting bogged down.

## Build your new habit

- **WHEN THIS HAPPENS...** Someone's given you an idea; you want to give advice; you're sure you know the answer; they haven't yet said "there is nothing else."
- **INSTEAD OF...** Going with the first (or second, or third) idea; sharing your own idea before they've finished; assuming you know the problem/solution; wrapping up early.
- **I WILL...** Ask, "And what else?"
