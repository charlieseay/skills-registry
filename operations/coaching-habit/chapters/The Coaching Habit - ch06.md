# 6. The Strategic Question — "If you're saying Yes to this, what are you saying No to?"

## Why "busy" isn't a measure of success

Being overcommitted is often humble-bragged as "a good busy." Michael Porter's framing anchors this chapter: **"The essence of strategy is choosing what not to do."** A Yes without a corresponding No is wishful thinking about your own capacity.

## Two types of No

Every real Yes creates two kinds of No:

1. **No of omission** — what's automatically excluded because you said Yes to this (the meeting you can't attend, the priority you can't chase).
2. **No of commission** — what you now have to actively stop doing, decline, or hand off to *make room* for the Yes.

Use the **3P Model** to find both:

| | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Projects** | What do you need to abandon or postpone? What meetings will you stop attending? What resources need to divert to the Yes? |
| **People** | What expectations need managing? What relationships will you let wither? What Drama Triangle dynamics will you exit? |
| **Patterns** | What habits do you need to break? What old stories/ambitions do you need to update? |

## Good and bad reasons to say Yes / No

| | Bad reason | Good reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | To get someone off the phone; because it'll make people like you; habit | You asked questions and got good answers; you know what you'll stop doing to make room; it's Great Work — real impact and meaning |
| **No** | You don't like the person; comfort; "attack is the best defense"; habit | You asked, got answers, and it's genuinely not a fit; you've thought about your core priorities |

## Saying Yes more slowly (instead of saying No outright)

Stay curious before committing — ask:
- "Why are you asking me?"
- "Whom else have you asked?"
- "When you say this is urgent, what do you mean?"
- "If I couldn't do all of this, but could do just a part, what part would you have me do?"
- "What do you want me to take off my plate so I can do this?"

## Softening a No

Create a "third point" — write the request down and say "I have to say No to *this*," not "I have to say No to *you*." Say Yes to the person, No to the task.

## The Playing to Win five strategic questions (Martin & Lafley)

For team/org-level strategy, not just individual capacity: *What is our winning aspiration? Where will we play? How will we win? What capabilities must be in place? What management systems are required?* — all in service of the same underlying Strategic Question: what will you say No to?

## Build your new habit

- **WHEN THIS HAPPENS...** Someone is about to add another commitment; they're saying Yes to everything without making a real choice; scope creep is visibly happening.
- **INSTEAD OF...** Assuming capacity is infinite; letting a Yes get added without naming the cost (Rescuer mode) or feeling forced into it (Victim mode).
- **I WILL...** Ask, "What will you say No to, to make this Yes rock-solid and real?"
