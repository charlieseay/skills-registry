# Patterns — situational playbook

Concrete "if this, then ask that" mappings, pulled from the book's own examples.

## They've listed 5 unrelated problems at once (Proliferation of Challenges)
→ "If you had to pick one of these to focus on, which one here would be the real challenge for you?"

## They're venting about a third person or situation (Coaching the Ghost)
→ "I think I understand some of what's going on with [X]. What's the real challenge here for you?"

## They're staying academic/abstract, lots of "we"/"us," no "I" (Abstractions & Generalizations)
→ "I have a sense of the overall challenge. What's the real challenge here for you?"

## Someone asks a fake rhetorical question disguised as advice ("Have you thought of...?", "What about...?")
→ Don't answer it as a real question. Instead, ask one of the Seven Essential Questions. If you still want to offer the idea, offer it plainly as an idea — not disguised as a question.

## Someone asks "How do I...?" and you feel the pull to answer
→ "That's a great question. I've got some ideas, which I'll share. But before I do — what are your first thoughts?" Then chain: "What else could you do?" ... "Is there anything else you could try here?" Only after that, offer your own idea if still needed.

## A conversation has stalled or feels stuck, and you're not sure why
→ "What do you want?" (Foundation Question) — often the real blocker is an unspoken want or need.

## Someone's about to commit to something new without freeing up capacity
→ "If you're saying Yes to this, what are you saying No to?" — then use the 3P Model (Projects/People/Patterns) to find both the No of omission and the No of commission.

## You don't know what's really going on and need a beat to think
→ "And what else?" — buys time while staying genuinely curious (don't fake it).

## Silence follows a question
→ Don't fill it. Wait at least 3-4 seconds. Silence is often the sound of someone actually thinking.

## A conversation or meeting is ending
→ "What was most useful here for you?" — the Learning Question, paired with whatever Kickstart Question opened the conversation (the Coaching Bookends).

## You're asked to say Yes quickly and don't want to
→ Say Yes more slowly: "Why are you asking me?" / "Whom else have you asked?" / "If I couldn't do all of this, what part would you have me do?"

## You need to say No without it landing as rejection of the person
→ Create a "third point" — name the request (write it down, point to it) and say No to *that*, not to them.
