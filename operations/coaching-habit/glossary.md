# Glossary — The Coaching Habit

**Advice Monster** — The habitual urge to leap into advice-giving/problem-solving mode the moment someone raises a challenge. Driven by genuine care, the comfort of certainty, and a desire to stay in control. The book's central obstacle to overcome.

**AGES model** (Josh Davis, NeuroLeadership Institute) — Four drivers of longer-term memory: Attention, Generation, Emotion, Spacing. "Generation" (creating your own connection to an idea) is why the Learning Question works.

**Coaching Bookends** — The Kickstart Question ("What's on your mind?") and the Learning Question ("What was most useful for you?") used together to open and close a conversation.

**Coaching for Performance vs. Coaching for Development** — Performance coaching fixes the immediate problem. Development coaching shifts focus to the *person* managing the problem — rarer, more powerful, cued by adding "for you" to questions.

**Deep Practice** (Dan Coyle, *The Talent Code*) — Three components of effective skill-building practice: (1) practicing small chunks of a bigger action, (2) heavy repetition in varied conditions, (3) mindful noticing and celebrating when it goes well.

**Double-loop learning** (Chris Argyris) — First loop: fix the problem. Second loop: create a learning moment about the problem itself. The Learning Question drives the second loop.

**Drama Triangle** (Stephen Karpman, from Transactional Analysis) — Three dysfunctional roles people cycle through under stress: **Victim** ("poor me," not my fault), **Persecutor** ("I'm surrounded by fools," not my fault, it's yours), **Rescuer** ("let me fix it," my responsibility not yours). Most people default to Rescuer.

**Foggy-fiers** — Three patterns that keep a conversation vague, addressed by the Focus Question: **Proliferation of Challenges** (too many problems at once), **Coaching the Ghost** (talking about a third party instead of yourself), **Abstractions & Generalizations** (staying high-level, no "I").

**New Habit Formula** — Three-part template for building any new behavior: **When this happens...** (the trigger) **instead of...** (the old habit) **I will...** (the new, <60-second behavior). Applied at the end of every chapter to each of the seven questions.

**Seven Essential Questions** — Kickstart, AWE, Focus, Foundation, Lazy, Strategic, Learning. See SKILL.md for the full table.

**TERA Quotient** — Four drivers of whether the brain reads a situation as safe/rewarding or dangerous: **Tribe** (with me or against me?), **Expectation** (do I know what's next?), **Rank** (relative status in the moment), **Autonomy** (do I get a say?).

**3P Model** — A framework for choosing what to focus on in a conversation: **Project** (the content/work), **People** (the relationship), **Patterns** (recurring behavior). Used both to open the Focus Question and to find the "No" side of the Strategic Question.

**Wants vs. Needs** (Marshall Rosenberg, Nonviolent Communication) — Wants are surface, tactical requests. Needs are the deeper human driver behind them. Nine universal needs (Manfred Max-Neef): Affection, Creation, Recreation, Freedom, Identity, Understanding, Participation, Protection, Subsistence.
