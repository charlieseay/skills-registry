# The Coaching Habit — Seven Essential Questions

## Philosophy

**Tell less, ask more.** The book's core behavior change: a little less advice, a little more curiosity. Not "never give advice" — advice has a place — but the default reflex of jumping to solve, fix, or answer is what this skill exists to interrupt.

**The Advice Monster.** The name for the urge to leap into solving/fixing/answering the moment someone raises a problem. It's driven by genuine care, status ("adding value"), and control — but it creates dependency, does other people's thinking for them, and usually solves the wrong problem. Naming it is the first step to managing it.

**Ask one question at a time.** Don't stack questions ("drive-by questioning"). Ask, then stop, then actually listen to the answer — including sitting through silence (people need 3-4 seconds to formulate a real answer; don't fill the gap).

**Stick to "What," avoid "Why."** "Why" questions put people on the defensive and pull toward fixing. Six of the seven questions start with "What."

## The Seven Essential Questions

| # | Question | Purpose | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Kickstart Question** — "What's on your mind?" | Opens a conversation focused and open — not too broad, not too narrow. Dissolves ossified agendas and small talk. | [chapters/The Coaching Habit - ch01.md](chapters/The%20Coaching%20Habit%20-%20ch01.md) |
| 2 | **The AWE Question** — "And what else?" | The best coaching question in the book. Surfaces more/better options; tames the Advice Monster; buys you thinking time. | 3 | **The Focus Question** — "What's the real challenge here for you?" | Cuts through the Foggy-fiers (Proliferation of Challenges, Coaching the Ghost, Abstractions & Generalizations) to find the actual problem, not the first problem. | [chapters/The Coaching Habit - ch03.md](chapters/The%20Coaching%20Habit%20-%20ch03.md) |
| 4 | **The Foundation Question** — "What do you want?" | Surfaces the want beneath the ask, and often the deeper need beneath the want. Raises the TERA Quotient (Tribe, Expectation, Rank, Autonomy). | [chapters/The Coaching Habit - ch04.md](chapters/The%20Coaching%20Habit%20-%20ch04.md) |
| 5 | **The Lazy Question** — "How can I help?" | Forces a direct, clear request instead of you guessing what "help" means and leaping into Rescuer mode. | [chapters/The Coaching Habit - ch05.md](chapters/The%20Coaching%20Habit%20-%20ch05.md) |
| 6 | **The Strategic Question** — "If you're saying Yes to this, what are you saying No to?" | Makes trade-offs explicit. A Yes without a No is not a real commitment — it's wishful capacity. | [chapters/The Coaching Habit - ch06.md](chapters/The%20Coaching%20Habit%20-%20ch06.md) |
| 7 | **The Learning Question** — "What was most useful for you?" | Closes the loop. Pairs with the Kickstart Question as "The Coaching Bookends." ## Quick reference: the 1-2-3 combination

For a fast, high-value coaching exchange, chain three questions:

1. **Open:** "What's on your mind?"
2. **Check in:** "Is there anything else on your mind?"
3. **Focus:** "So what's the real challenge here for you?"
4. **Deepen:** "And what else [is the real challenge here for you]?"
5. **Probe again:** "Is there anything else?"
6. **Land it:** "So... what's the real challenge here for you?"

## The Coaching Bookends

Start every meaningful conversation with the **Kickstart Question** and end it with the **Learning Question**. This pair alone — even without the other five — changes how conversations open and close.

## When to use each question (situational index)

- **Conversation hasn't really started / feels like small talk** → Kickstart Question
- **First answer they gave is probably not the only or best one** → AWE Question
- **They've listed 5 problems, or they're talking about someone else's problem, or they're staying abstract** → Focus Question
- **You're not sure what they're actually asking you for** → Foundation Question or Lazy Question
- **Someone asks "How do I...?" and you feel the urge to answer** → Lazy Question, then AWE Question on their own ideas
- **Someone's about to say Yes to something new without freeing up capacity** → Strategic Question
- **A conversation or meeting is wrapping up** → Learning Question
