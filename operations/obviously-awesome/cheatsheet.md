# Obviously Awesome — One-Page Cheatsheet

## The Six Components of Effective Positioning (in order)

1. **Competitive Alternatives** — What would customers do if you didn't exist? (spreadsheets, hire someone, do nothing, other product)
2. **Unique Attributes** — What do you have that they don't? (technical features, business model, expertise, delivery method)
3. **Value (+ Proof)** — What's the real-world benefit? (fact-based; customer/data/third-party proof required)
4. **Target Market** — Who cares most about this value? (specific segment, not "everyone")
5. **Market Category** — What frame helps customers understand? (CRM, data warehouse, team chat, etc.)
6. **Relevant Trends** — Why does this matter right now? (AI, mobile-first, direct-to-consumer, etc.)

**Golden rule:** Work through in order. Each depends on the previous one.

---

## The 10-Step Positioning Process

| Step | Do This | Ask This |
|------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Identify best customers | Who loves us most? Quick buying, high usage, refer us, act as reference? |
| 2 | Assemble team | Business leader + marketing + sales + product + customer success + external facilitator |
| 3 | Surface baggage, align vocab | What history is baked into how we see this? What did we mean to build vs. what is it? |
| 4 | List competitive alternatives | If we didn't exist, what would best customers do? Focus on what *they* see, not what *we* see. |
| 5 | Isolate unique attributes | What do we have that competitive alternatives don't? (Keep to 3-5 key ones) |
| 6 | Map attributes to value | For each attribute, what real customer benefit? Proof required (not opinion) |
| 7 | Determine target segment | Who cares most about this value? What makes them different? How do we find them? |
| 8 | Choose market category | Which frame makes our strengths obvious? (existing category or new one?) |
| 9 | Layer on trend | Is there a current trend our segment cares about? Makes this more urgent? (optional but often useful) |
| 10 | Capture & distribute | Document all six components. Make it actionable for every team. Live doc, not PDF. |

---

## Quick Diagnosis: "What's Wrong With Our Positioning?"

| Symptom | Likely Root Cause | Fix |
|---------|------------------|-----|
| Happy customers, confused prospects | Wrong segment, category, or value unclear | Steps 7-8 (target market + category) |
| Long sales cycles, low close rates | Wrong competitive frame or category | Steps 4, 8 (alternatives, category) |
| High churn after purchase | Wrong customers attracted (not best-fit segment) | Steps 1, 7 (best customers, target segment) |
| Losing to unknown competitors | Missing competitive alternatives | Step 4 (competitive alternatives) |
| Product roadmap drifts from positioning | Positioning not strategic; no enforcement | Make positioning explicit in roadmap decisions |
| Teams interpret positioning differently | Poor documentation or no enforcement | Step 10 (clear documentation + training) |
| Positioning is 2+ years old, market shifted | Baggage blocking; no refresh cycle | Revisit Steps 1, 4, 7, 8 annually or when market shifts |

## Red Flags (Stop and Reconsider)

🚩 **Value statements are opinions, not facts**
- Bad: "Beautifully designed," "incredibly powerful," "loved by customers"
- Good: "Reduces reporting time by 60% (customer data)," "Gartner Magic Quadrant leader"

🚩 **Positioning is all about features, not customer problems**
- Bad: "We have a patented algorithm"
- Good: "Our algorithm enables faster decisions (20 min vs. 2 hours) because..."

🚩 **Target segment is "everyone"**
- Sign: You haven't narrowed to who cares most. Go back to Steps 1 & 7.

🚩 **You're trying to position in a mature category against the leader**
- Alternatives: (a) Reposition in different category (b) Find subcategory where you're strongest (c) Challenge the leader if you have superior proof

🚩 **Multiple trends in your messaging**
- Keep to one trend, maximum two. More = confusing.

🚩 **Positioning statement sounds like marketing fluff**
- Should sound boring/technical, not clever. If it's clever, it's hiding something unclear.

## How Each Team Uses Positioning

**Marketing:** Messaging, audience targeting, content strategy, website/brand strategy

**Sales:** Target accounts, pitch deck structure, objection handling, sales training/tools

**Product:** Roadmap prioritization, feature rationale, what to build vs. deprioritize, design focus

**Customer Success:** Onboarding, expansion strategy, churn risk identification, reference program

**Hiring:** Job descriptions focused on skills needed to execute positioning, interview messaging

## When to Refresh Positioning

- ✅ Annually (light review; are best customers same as last year?)
- ✅ When market shifts significantly (new competitor, new trend, regulatory change)
- ✅ When growth has hit a ceiling (sales cycle increasing, close rates declining)
- ✅ When entering a new market/vertical (different customers = different positioning)
- ✅ When you realize product has evolved beyond original positioning

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

❌ **Positioning Statements** (traditional "FOR... IS A... UNLIKE..." format)
→ Start with components instead; statements come last, not first

❌ **Feature-First Positioning**
→ Start with customer alternatives, not features

❌ **Positioning Investor vs. Customers the Same**
→ Investors want vision; customers want solution. Different pitches.

❌ **Positioning in Marketing Only**
→ Positioning is strategy; must be cross-functional

❌ **Positioning Document Gathering Dust**
→ Make it live, reference it constantly, update 1-2x/year

❌ **Forgetting Market Category Matters**
→ Category choice is strategic; it determines competitive set and customer assumptions

---

## Key Metrics of Good Positioning

✅ Sales cycle is shortening or stayed short
✅ Close rates are above target (industry-typical or higher)
✅ Customer acquisition cost is stable or improving
✅ Churn is low and stable
✅ Referral rate is high (customers recommend you)
✅ Price sensitivity is low (rarely haggle on price)
✅ NPS is high and focused on same value themes
✅ Product roadmap reinforces positioning
✅ New employees understand positioning in first week
✅ All teams talk about positioning in similar ways

---

## One More Thing

**Positioning is not static.** Markets evolve. Customers change. You learn. Refresh positioning 1-2x per year or when market shifts. A live, evolving positioning strategy beats a perfect positioning document that's 3 years old.
