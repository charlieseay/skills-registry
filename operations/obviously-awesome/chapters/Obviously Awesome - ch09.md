# Step 9: Layer On a Trend (But Be Careful)

## Core Idea
Trends are secondary context that help customers understand why your product matters *right now*. They're not market categories (which help customers understand what you are) but characteristics that make your offering urgent and strategic. Used wisely, trends amplify positioning. Used carelessly, they confuse it or date your messaging.

## Trends vs. Categories: Not the Same

**Market Category** (Step 8):
- Tells customers what the product is
- Is relatively stable (CRM is CRM for decades)
- Creates assumptions about features, competitors, pricing
- Example: "We're a CRM"

**Trend** (Step 9):
- Tells customers why the product matters *now*
- Is moving/evolving (AI is new, blockchain is new, mobile-first, remote-first, etc.)
- Adds urgency or strategic context
- Example: "We're an AI-powered CRM" or "We're a remote-first CRM"

Trends are temporary context to explain why customers should pay attention. Categories are stable context to explain what you are.

## When Trends Help Positioning

Trends work best when they:
- **Align with your target market's priorities:** Your segment already understands the trend and sees it as important
- **Make your solution more strategic:** "AI-powered invoicing" sounds more strategic than "invoicing software"
- **Help you compete:** The trend explains why you matter now when you didn't matter before
- **Are validated by multiple sources:** Not just one analyst or one blog post, but clear market movement

**Sampler (Product Sampling) Example:**
- Base category: "Product sampling"
- Target market: Consumer brands
- Trend: "Direct-to-consumer revolution" (brands leaving retail, building own relationships with customers)
- Trend positioning: "Direct-to-consumer sampling"
- Result: Buyers immediately understood this aligns with their strategic priority

The trend didn't change what Sampler is (still product sampling). But it added urgency by connecting to the brand executives' strategic focus.

**Dunford's Database Company:**
- Base category: "Data warehouse"
- Target market: Companies doing data analysis
- Trend (at the time): "Big data" (companies suddenly had more data than ever, analysis was strategically important)
- Trend positioning: "Big data warehouse" or "data warehouse for the big data era"
- Result: Made the solution more timely and urgent

## Common Trends in Tech

- **Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning:** "AI-powered," "machine learning-driven"
- **Mobile-First:** "Mobile-first," "mobile app for..."
- **Remote-First / Async:** "Built for remote teams," "asynchronous-first"
- **Real-Time:** "Real-time analytics," "real-time collaboration"
- **Direct-to-Consumer:** "DTC-first," "direct-to-consumer..."
- **Developer-Friendly:** "Developer experience," "built for engineers"
- **Privacy / Security:** "Privacy-first," "zero-knowledge," "end-to-end encrypted"
- **Sustainability / ESG:** "Sustainable," "carbon-neutral," "ethical"

## How to Find Relevant Trends for Your Positioning

**1. Look at what your target market is reading, discussing, investing in.**
- What are they saying is a priority?
- What market shifts are they worried about?
- What new capabilities are they trying to adopt?
- What are competitors claiming?

**2. Ask: Does this trend make my solution more valuable or urgent to them?**
- If your segment cares about AI, and your product has AI capabilities, that's a relevant trend
- If your segment is mostly unconcerned with AI, positioning with AI is noise

**3. Validate it's a real trend, not a fleeting buzzword.**
- Multiple analysts mentioning it? Good sign
- Continued investment by companies in this area? Good sign
- Just one blog post? Probably too early
- Multiple years of similar discussion? Might be too late/obvious

**4. Ask: Does this trend make sense for how we position against competitive alternatives?**
- If our alternative is "do it manually," claiming "AI-powered" might not matter (they still beat manual)
- If our alternative is "competitor who's also AI-powered," both of us can claim the trend (not differentiating)

## Trends That Backfire

**Trends that confuse rather than clarify:**

- **Using too many trends:** "AI-powered, mobile-first, real-time, privacy-focused, direct-to-consumer platform" tells customers nothing.
- **Choosing a trend your segment doesn't care about:** Positioning as "AI-powered invoicing" to accountants who just want invoicing to work
- **Trends that conflict with your category:** "Blockchain CRM" (blockchain buyers don't think blockchain is a CRM feature)
- **Outdated trends:** "Cloud-based" used to be a differentiating trend; now it's table stakes. Claiming it makes you sound old.

## Trends as Justification for Positioning Change

Trends can be the reason to reposition, not just a modifier on existing positioning.

**Example: Wave Accounting**
- Original positioning: "Accounting software"
- Market trend: Small businesses shifting from desktop accounting to cloud + expanded financial services
- New positioning: "Financial software" (not accounting; accountants use it, but financial services apply to all business owners)
- Trend context: Direct-to-consumer trends, digital payments, fintech transformation
- Result: Made room for payroll, payments, credit products under one umbrella

If the market is shifting around your category (e.g., "accounting" is narrowing as "financial services" expands), trends can signal it's time to reposition.

## Step 9 Checklist

- [ ] Do our best-fit customers already understand this trend?
- [ ] Does this trend make our solution more strategic or urgent for them?
- [ ] Can we back up our trend claim with real product capabilities?
- [ ] Does this trend align with how we position against our competitive set?
- [ ] Have we used just one trend, not multiple?
- [ ] Does this trend feel current and relevant, not dated or buzzword-y?
- [ ] Is this trend likely to be relevant for at least the next 2-3 years?

If you answer no to any, reconsider the trend or drop it.

## Red Flags

- **You can't explain why the trend matters to your segment:** The trend is noise, not useful context.
- **Multiple trends in your positioning:** Confusing and unfocused. Pick one, max two.
- **Trend is newer/flashier than your actual value:** Positioning is chasing hype instead of solving problems.
- **Sales uses the trend but ignores the category:** Customers get confused about what you actually are.
- **The trend was obvious 3 years ago:** You're late to claiming it; everyone else owns it now.
