# Step 2: Form a Positioning Team

## Core Idea
Positioning is a business strategy exercise, not a marketing exercise. It requires cross-functional alignment from the people who actually execute against the positioning (product, sales, marketing, customer success). The business leader must drive it. Teams expose hidden assumptions and ensure the final positioning is adopted across the entire company, not filed away unused.

## Why Team Effort Matters

Positioning doesn't live in one department. It flows through every group:
- **Marketing:** Messaging, audience targeting, campaign strategy
- **Sales & BD:** Target customer segmentation, account strategy, sales motion
- **Customer Success:** Onboarding strategy, account expansion, retention
- **Product & Development:** Roadmap prioritization, feature decisions, long-term vision

A positioning effort that isn't cross-functional and business-led will fail because one team will sabotage it through poor execution.

## Who Must Be in the Room

**1. The Business Leader**
The person responsible for the product or business unit must be driving this effort and seen as owning it. In startups: CEO/founders. In larger companies: division leader, business unit head, or occasionally CMO/CPO if they own the business P&L.

*Why:* Positioning is strategy. It requires authority and credibility. If the business leader doesn't visibly own it, teams won't believe it's real.

**2. One Senior Person from Each Functional Area**
- Marketing (for customer perspective and messaging)
- Sales (for sales motion and customer objections)
- Customer Success (for churn patterns and expansion hooks)
- Product (for feasibility and feature rationale)

*Why:* Each group sees customers at different points in the buyer journey and holds different perspectives on competitive alternatives, differentiators, and best-fit customer characteristics. You need all viewpoints.

**3. NOT: Everyone from those functions**
Having one or two senior representatives per function is enough. Too many people in the room (more than 12) makes it hard for each group's voice to be heard and creates domination by whoever speaks loudest.

## Who Should Facilitate

**Bring in an external facilitator** (an experienced positioning consultant, not internal staff).

Why:
- **Balanced discussion:** An outsider ensures everyone's voice is heard and stops one or two functions from dominating.
- **Challenge assumptions:** An outsider can gently push back on "that's just how we've always thought about it"—which insiders rarely do to each other.
- **Focus and efficiency:** Positioning workshops with external facilitation consistently move faster and produce better alignment than internal-only attempts.

If you cannot afford external facilitation, designate someone from outside the core business (HR, legal, finance) to run the meeting with that mandate.

## Outputs of a Good Positioning Team

A good positioning team meeting exposes:
- **Hidden assumptions:** Why does marketing think the competitive set is Product X, Y, Z while sales thinks it's spreadsheets and manual work?
- **Departmental blind spots:** Product might not realize sales spends half every meeting explaining what the product does. Marketing might not know customers churn because they bought for the wrong reasons.
- **Different customer understanding:** Sales and customer success talk to customers all day; they often see patterns product and marketing miss.
- **Organizational misalignment:** Once you position deliberately, you realize many internal processes (hiring, onboarding, roadmap) are working against that positioning.

## Red Flags

- **Positioning driven by marketing only:** Will fail to gain product/sales buy-in and won't inform roadmap decisions.
- **CEO drives alone, doesn't involve team:** Creates resentment and lack of ownership. Teams will ignore it.
- **Too many people in the room:** No clear voice; discussion devolves into competing agendas.
- **Lacking sales or customer success:** Positioning will miss critical customer insight about objections and churn.
- **Facilitator is internal to the business:** Lacks authority to challenge executives; easier for people to dismiss feedback.
