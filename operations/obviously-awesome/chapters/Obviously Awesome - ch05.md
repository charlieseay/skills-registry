# Step 5: Isolate Your Unique Attributes or Features

## Core Idea
Unique attributes are the capabilities, features, or characteristics you have that your competitive alternatives don't. They're your "secret sauce." But here's the critical part: something is only unique when compared to *specific alternatives*. An attribute that makes you better than a spreadsheet might make you identical to a competitor. Attributes are meaningless until you compare them to the alternatives your customers actually consider.

## What Counts as a Unique Attribute?

For tech/software companies:
- **Technical features:** "We have a patented algorithm that returns results 100x faster"
- **Delivery model:** "We're cloud-based and on-premise" vs. competitor who's only cloud
- **Business model:** "You lease instead of buy" (Rent the Runway vs. traditional retail)
- **Integrations:** "We integrate with 50+ tools out of the box"

For service businesses (agencies, consultancies, custom dev):
- **Expertise combination:** "We're the only Python dev shop with deep retail point-of-sale experience"
- **Industry experience:** "We've worked with 12 international banks and understand their compliance needs"
- **Process/methodology:** "We use design thinking + agile, not waterfall"
- **Team credentials:** "Our team includes a former VP of Sales at Slack"

For physical products or companies:
- **Proprietary process:** "Our manufacturing uses technique X"
- **Supply chain:** "We source directly from ethical suppliers"
- **Design/aesthetics:** "Innovative form factor that competitors don't have"

## How to Isolate Attributes

**Start with your best-fit customers (Step 1) and the alternatives they named (Step 4).**

Ask your team:
1. **What capabilities do we have that the alternatives don't?**
2. **What can we do that they can't do?**
3. **For each competitive alternative group, what specific technical, operational, or organizational advantage do we have over them?**

For example, if alternatives are "spreadsheets + manual work":
- Automation (you eliminate manual entry)
- Real-time data (spreadsheets are static)
- Collaboration (multiple people can work simultaneously)
- Data validation (you prevent user error)
- Visualization (built-in reporting vs. pivot tables)

If alternative is "use our existing database," your advantages might be:
- Speed (your query engine is 100x faster)
- Cost (you're cheaper than licensing another tool)
- Ease of setup (theirs requires DBA expertise; yours is self-service)

## Don't Start Here

This is a step that often tempts teams to start the positioning process. **Don't.** Here's why:

- **You'll miss alternatives.** If you list attributes first ("we have a patented algorithm"), you're implicitly choosing which alternatives those attributes matter against. You haven't researched what customers actually see as alternatives.
- **You'll overweight features you love.** Product teams love the clever technical features. But customers often care about different things.
- **You'll end up defending the status quo.** "We have attributes X, Y, Z because we built them that way in version 1" leads to defensive positioning that doesn't evolve.

Work through Steps 1-4 first. Then ask: "Given these customers, these alternatives they consider, what do we have that those alternatives don't?"

## The Pitfall: Assumed Uniqueness

A common mistake: claiming something is unique when it's not—relative to alternatives customers consider.

**Example:** Your CRM has a mobile app. You call it a unique attribute. But your top three competitive alternatives are Salesforce, HubSpot, and doing it in spreadsheets/Outlook. The first two also have mobile apps (not unique relative to them). Mobile matters vs. spreadsheets, but not vs. direct product competitors. This gets confusing in positioning.

**Better approach:** List attributes separately for each alternative group:
- **Vs. spreadsheets/manual work:** Automation, real-time data, reporting, collaboration
- **Vs. Salesforce:** Lower cost, easier setup, better for X workflow, lighter interface
- **Vs. HubSpot:** Integrates with Y tools, specialized for Z industry

## Quantity vs. Quality

Don't list every possible attribute. Focus on the ones that matter to your best-fit customers and that differentiate you from the alternatives they consider.

April typically sees teams converge on **3-5 key unique attributes.** More than that and you're diluting focus. Your positioning needs to be clear and memorable.

## Red Flags

- **Attributes list looks like a feature tour.** Too many items, too technical. Trim to what really matters.
- **Team keeps saying "but we also have..."** You're adding lower-value attributes. Cut ruthlessly.
- **Attributes sound like marketing fluff ("best-in-class," "innovative")** rather than concrete capabilities. Be specific.
- **You can't explain why these attributes matter to your best-fit customers.** Your attributes are disconnected from customer reality. Go back to Step 1.
- **Half the attributes also apply to competitive alternatives.** You're calling things unique that aren't. Be more selective.
