# Step 1: Understand the Customers Who Love Your Product

## Core Idea
The best market positioning starts by studying your happiest customers, not your product features. They reveal what your offering truly is, who it naturally appeals to, and why your assumptions about the market might be wrong. Begin here because these best-fit customers hold the key to everything that follows.

## Why Start Here

Positioning has circular dependencies: the best market category depends on target customers and the value they care about. Value depends on differentiators. Differentiators depend on which alternatives you compare against. Alternatives depend on market category. This circularity breaks when you start with empirical fact: which customers bought quickest, stayed longest, referred others, and loved you most?

April calls this "finding things that work, then deciphering why." Instead of guessing at positioning, look at:
- Which marketing campaigns drew the most high-quality leads?
- What events had the best attendee quality?
- Which pieces of content were consumed by ideal buyers?
- Which customer cohorts have the lowest churn and highest lifetime value?

These questions lead to the happiest customers. Once you narrow in on them, ask: What was it about our offering that made them so happy? What characteristics do they share that made them a perfect fit?

## The Trap: Starting With Product Instead

Your first instinct might be to start with features and positioning. Don't. Here's why:
- **Product-first leads to investor thinking, not customer thinking.** Investors care about your 5-10 year vision and market size. Customers care about solving today's problem right now.
- **You unconsciously pick the wrong competitive set.** If you start with features, you're implicitly comparing yourself to a set of competitors you assume are your competitors—often other startups fighting for funding in your space. But your customers have never heard of them. They compare you to what they use today (Excel, hiring, doing nothing).
- **You reinforce status quo positioning.** The default positioning based on what you set out to build almost always favors market leaders and blind spots you to alternative framings.

## How to Identify Best-Fit Customers

Create a short list (8-12) of your best customers by asking:
- Did they understand your product quickly?
- Did they buy from you quickly (short sales cycle)?
- Do they use your product heavily and evangelize it?
- Did they refer you to other companies?
- Do they act as references for your sales team?

These are your model customers—the type you want to clone.

## What If You Don't Have Super-Happy Customers Yet?

If you're early-stage and still testing product-market fit, hold off on tightening positioning. Instead:
- Cast a wide net and test your offering on diverse audiences.
- Watch which types of customers gravitate to you and love you.
- Look for patterns: Do bigger businesses love it more than smaller ones? Do specific industries stand out? Do happy customers share characteristics?
- Think of your product as a fishing net: you have a theory about what you'll catch (grouper), but until you fish, you don't know. If you start catching tuna, move to the tuna spot.

Positioning broadly as a "general solution" when you have little market experience keeps options open until patterns emerge.

## Using Best-Fit Customers as Your North Star

Once you've identified best customers, they become the reference point for the entire 10-step process:
- Step 4 (competitive alternatives): What would these customers do if you didn't exist?
- Step 5 (unique attributes): What features matter most to them?
- Step 7 (target market characteristics): What do they have in common?

The best-fit customer list is your data point. Everything else is working backward from that data to build the positioning narrative.

## Red Flags

- **Heterogeneous customer base with no clear pattern:** You likely don't have true positioning yet. Broaden your market and look for patterns to emerge.
- **Customers bought for reasons unrelated to your intended positioning:** This is common and valuable information. You may need to reposition to match how customers actually use you.
- **Customer success metrics show high churn with new customer segments:** The segment you're targeting may not be your actual best-fit. Return to this step and identify the right segment.
