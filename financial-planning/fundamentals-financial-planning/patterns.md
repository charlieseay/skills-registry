# Financial Planning Patterns and Situational Playbook

## Pattern 1: The Underinsured Client

**Recognition**
- Life insurance that would replace less than 5 years of income
- No disability insurance despite dependent family
- Inadequate umbrella liability coverage relative to assets
- Health insurance with high deductibles and limited coverage

**Root Cause**
- Cost concerns and budget constraints
- Underestimation of risk ("it won't happen to me")
- Complexity of insurance landscape
- Previous advisor neglected risk management
- Life changes without insurance review (marriage, children, promotion)

**Advisor Response**
1. **Calculate True Need** - Specific coverage amounts based on income replacement, debt payoff, education funding, final expenses
2. **Address Objections** - Acknowledge cost, show ROI, explain catastrophic impact of underinsurance
3. **Staged Approach** - Start with most critical (life, disability), add umbrella and other coverage as budget allows
4. **Annual Review** - Ensure coverage keeps pace with income and assets
5. **Coordination** - Ensure beneficiaries, coverage amounts, and deductibles integrated

## Pattern 2: The Asset Concentration Risk

**Recognition**
- Single business comprises >50% of net worth
- Stock compensation from employer creates concentrated position
- Real estate holdings in single geographic area
- Inherited illiquid assets undiversified

**Root Cause**
- Success in single area creates natural concentration
- Business owner pride/attachment to company
- Insider knowledge making business seem safer than market
- Illiquidity making sale/diversification difficult
- Tax implications of diversification

**Advisor Response**
1. **Quantify Risk** - Show concentration relative to total assets and portfolio theory
2. **Explore Options** - Diversification, hedging strategies, options strategies, sale timeline
3. **Address Emotional Barriers** - Acknowledge business attachment, legacy concerns
4. **Tax Planning** - Structured approach minimizing tax impact (exchange funds, gifts, sale strategies)
5. **Implement Gradually** - Dollar-cost averaging diversification over time, diversify new contributions
6. **Legacy Planning** - Integrate business succession with diversification strategy

## Pattern 3: The Retirement Income Insufficiency

**Recognition**
- Projected retirement assets insufficient to maintain lifestyle
- Savings rate inadequate to bridge gap
- Late-start saving (age 50+ with minimal retirement funds)
- Pension reduction or elimination from expected plan

**Root Cause**
- Career disruptions (unemployment, relocation, job changes)
- Family expenses (education, supporting parents, medical)
- Poor savings discipline early career
- Investment losses from market downturns or poor choices
- Lifestyle inflation preventing savings accumulation

**Advisor Response**
1. **Realistic Projection** - Run detailed cash flow projections showing income sources, expenses, shortfall
2. **Goal Adjustment** - Discuss retirement timing, lifestyle changes, geographic arbitrage (lower cost location)
3. **Savings Acceleration** - Maximize contributions (catch-up provisions), use windfalls, adjust budget
4. **Investment Review** - Ensure allocation appropriate for time horizon, revisit risk tolerance
5. **Retirement Planning** - Part-time work in retirement, phased transition, delaying Social Security for higher benefits
6. **Family Communication** - Address expectations with spouse/children about retirement lifestyle

## Pattern 4: The Blended Family Conflict

**Recognition**
- Multiple marriages with children from different relationships
- Estate plan conflicting priorities (current spouse vs. adult children)
- Unequal asset distribution creating family tension
- Business succession complicated by family dynamics
- Healthcare/guardianship decisions unclear

**Root Cause**
- Difficulty reconciling current spouse needs with children's inheritance expectations
- Estate plan not updated from prior marriage
- Family communication avoidance (emotionally difficult conversations)
- Unclear priorities and values
- Professional (attorney, CPA) silos without coordination

**Advisor Response**
1. **Private Conversations** - Meet with each spouse/key stakeholder separately to understand concerns
2. **Values Clarification** - Determine non-negotiables vs. flexible items
3. **Options Presentation** - QTIP trusts, equalization strategies, separate wills, prenups, life insurance
4. **Facilitation** - Family meeting with advisor/attorney to discuss framework and seek consensus
5. **Documentation** - Clear written plan with rationale reducing post-mortem conflict
6. **Communication** - Appropriate family discussion about inheritance (or respect privacy if preferred)

## Pattern 5: The Sudden Wealth Anxiety

**Recognition**
- Large inheritance, bonus, business sale, or lawsuit settlement received
- Paralysis about where/how to invest
- Family pressure and "advice" from multiple sources
- Fear of making wrong decision
- Lifestyle inflation pressure (new house, cars, spending)

**Root Cause**
- Overwhelm from large sum and options
- Responsibility anxiety
- Pressure from family, friends, professionals
- Emotional attachment to time-limited opportunity
- Fear of taxes or losing money

**Advisor Response**
1. **Initial Stabilization** - Deposit in safe money market/savings, resist pressure for immediate decisions
2. **Tax Planning** - Calculate tax liabilities and coordinate with accountant, avoid surprises
3. **Organized Assessment** - Gather family input (if couple), clarify goals, identify priorities
4. **Staged Deployment** - Allocate to goals systematically rather than all-at-once
5. **Education** - Explain investment choices, diversification, expected returns
6. **Behavioral Coaching** - Address psychological barriers, family pressure, emotional spending
7. **Annual Review** - Check plan remains on track, adjust if circumstances change

## Pattern 6: The Denial of Aging

**Recognition**
- Client approaching/past normal retirement age with no retirement plan
- No succession plan for business despite age/health concerns
- Healthcare and long-term care planning completely absent
- Estate documents from 20+ years ago
- No conversation with family about care preferences

**Root Cause**
- Psychological difficulty accepting aging
- Work provides identity and social connection
- Fear of loss of control/independence
- Difficulty having mortality conversations
- Business success creating false sense of invulnerability

**Advisor Response**
1. **Compassionate Conversation** - Acknowledge difficulty, normalize aging concerns
2. **Phased Transition** - Part-time work, mentoring role, gradual transition vs. cliff retirement
3. **Business Planning** - Successor development, sale timeline, transition structure
4. **Incapacity Planning** - Power of attorney, healthcare directives, guardianship discussions
5. **Long-Term Care** - Insurance options, self-insurance vs. insurance trade-offs, family discussion
6. **Legacy Clarification** - Values, mentoring, charitable interests, family time priorities
7. **Family Involvement** - Gradual inclusion of successor/heirs in planning and decisions

## Pattern 7: The High-Expense Lifestyle Inconsistent with Income

**Recognition**
- Expenses exceed income despite strong absolute income level
- Limited savings despite high earning capacity
- Debt accumulation despite wealth
- No emergency fund despite high income
- Lifestyle must be maintained (status anxiety)

**Root Cause**
- Lifestyle inflation keeping pace with income growth
- Peer/social pressure (wealthy friend circle spending norms)
- Identity tied to spending (cars, home, clothing brand)
- Delayed or unrealistic financial consequence recognition
- Lack of budget/tracking

**Advisor Response**
1. **Non-Judgmental Assessment** - Detailed expense tracking without judgment
2. **Reality Check** - Projections showing income insufficiency for goals if trends continue
3. **Value Clarification** - Distinguish truly important vs. habitual/status-driven spending
4. **Graduated Approach** - Identify discretionary cuts without sacrificing what matters
5. **Automation** - Automatic transfers to savings before spending budget calculated
6. **Accountability** - Regular review of spending, progress toward goals
7. **Behavioral Coaching** - Address status anxiety, peer pressure, identity issues; find identity in other areas

## Pattern 8: The Inherited Asset Mismanagement

**Recognition**
- Inherited assets held in inappropriate investments (concentrated stock, outdated bonds)
- No active management or review of inherited accounts
- Beneficiary designation problems (ex-spouse, minor, deceased person)
- Tax-inefficient positioning (full basis assets in taxable account)
- Advisor/custodian relationship lapsed

**Root Cause**
- Emotional difficulty managing inheritance
- Lack of knowledge about investments
- Procrastination on technical aspects
- Inherited from different advisor with poor relationship
- No planning for management post-inheritance

**Advisor Response**
1. **Empathetic Transition** - Acknowledge emotional aspects of inheritance, not just financial
2. **Comprehensive Review** - Asset-by-asset assessment for appropriateness, tax efficiency, concentration risk
3. **Rebalancing Plan** - Systematic repositioning (sometimes gradual to minimize tax/emotional impact)
4. **Beneficiary Fix** - Correct any designation issues and ensure alignment
5. **Tax Optimization** - Use step-up in basis, coordinate with other holdings
6. **Ongoing Management** - Regular review and rebalancing as circumstances change

## Pattern 9: The Estate Tax Avoidance Opportunity

**Recognition**
- Combined net worth with spouse near/exceeds exemption limit ($13.61M in 2024)
- No coordinated gift strategy despite opportunity
- Outdated plan from pre-exemption increase
- Business/real estate creates concentration and estate planning need
- Family members at different wealth levels

**Root Cause**
- Exemption changes creating planning opportunity window
- Complexity and cost of estate planning preventing action
- Reluctance to "give away" money during life
- Outdated advice or plan no longer appropriate
- Uncertainty about best strategy

**Advisor Response**
1. **Exemption Review** - Update on current exemption, sunset risk (2025), planning window
2. **Options Presentation** - Outright gifts, dynasty trusts, intentionally defective grantor trusts, charitable strategies
3. **Tax Projection** - Show tax savings from different approaches
4. **Psychologically Gradual** - Annual exclusion gifts feel less like "giving away"
5. **Coordination** - Spouse gifting strategies, business succession integration
6. **Attorney Referral** - Proper legal structure essential (DIY or cheap plans often ineffective)

## Pattern 10: The Advisor-Resistant Client

**Recognition**
- Client won't act on recommendations despite agreement
- Doesn't return calls or follow up on decisions
- Procrastinates on important changes
- Defensive when progress questioned
- Relationship deteriorating or terminated

**Root Cause**
- Underlying fear (of change, mistakes, loss of control)
- Perfectionism preventing good enough decision
- Grief/loss in life change (job loss, divorce, death)
- Distrust of advisor or advisors generally
- Overwhelm from complexity or volume of recommendations
- Advisor approach not matching client personality/style

**Advisor Response**
1. **Honest Conversation** - Address resistance directly and kindly
2. **Explore Barriers** - Understand fears, objections, real vs. stated concerns
3. **Simplify** - Reduce volume, prioritize top 2-3 most important actions
4. **Involve Client** - Create plan collaboratively rather than prescriptive
5. **Match Style** - Adapt communication to client personality (analytical wants details, intuitive wants overview)
6. **Smaller Steps** - Break large changes into achievable steps with wins
7. **Permission to Vary** - Acknowledge multiple right answers, not one perfect path
