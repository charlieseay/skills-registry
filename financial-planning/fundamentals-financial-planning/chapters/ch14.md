# Chapter 14. Estate Planning

## Overview of Estate Planning

Estate planning is the process of organizing financial and personal affairs to achieve life and death objectives efficiently and in accordance with wishes. It addresses both the healthy life (incapacity planning) and what happens after death (property transfer).

### Goals of Estate Planning
- Transfer property according to wishes
- Minimize estate, gift, and income taxes
- Avoid probate and preserve privacy
- Provide for minor children's guardianship
- Plan for incapacity and healthcare wishes
- Protect assets from creditors
- Facilitate family communication about wealth

## Probate and Alternatives

### What is Probate?
- Court process validating will and transferring property
- Supervised by probate court
- Generally public record
- Takes 1-2+ years
- Costs 3-7% of estate value
- Pays creditors' claims and taxes

### Probate Advantages
- Orderly process under court supervision
- Creditor claims limited time window (typically 3-9 months)
- Clear legal authority for fiduciary actions
- Some protection against will contests

### Probate Disadvantages
- Time-consuming process
- Significant costs (attorney fees, court costs, executor fees)
- Public record (privacy loss)
- Delays asset distribution
- Inflexible timing

### Probate Avoidance Strategies

**Revocable Living Trust**
- Trust holds assets during life and transfers at death without probate
- Trustor maintains full control during lifetime
- Successor trustee manages/distributes at incapacity or death
- Private (not public record)
- Avoids probate for trust-held assets
- Requires funding (retitling assets)

**Non-Probate Property Transfers**
- Life insurance proceeds (named beneficiary)
- Retirement accounts (named beneficiary)
- Payable-on-death bank accounts
- Transfer-on-death investment accounts
- Property owned jointly with right of survivorship

**Pour-Over Will**
- Coordinates with revocable living trust
- Catches assets not in trust
- Directs probate assets to trust
- Back-up to trust plan

## Will and Testament

### Purposes of a Will
- Direct disposition of probate assets
- Designate executor (personal representative)
- Appoint guardians for minor children (critical function)
- Provide for specific bequests
- Establish testamentary trusts for minor heirs

### Essential Elements
- Testator capacity (understand nature and extent of property, family, disposition plan)
- Testator intent (freely made, not under duress)
- Execution formalities (signed, dated, witnessed, notarized)
- Clear disposition provisions
- Executor designation

### Common Will Provisions
- Family definition
- Asset disposition (specific and residual)
- Executor and successor executor
- Guardian for minor children
- Spendthrift provisions
- Testamentary trust creation

### Limitations
- Only transfers probate assets
- Subject to court process and fees
- Public record
- Revoked or modified by subsequent will

## Trusts

### Revocable Living Trust (Grantor Trust)
**During Grantor's Life:**
- Grantor creates, funds, and controls trust
- Grantor is trustee or co-trustee
- Grantor retains full control and income
- Revocable (can be changed or cancelled)
- Taxed as grantor for income tax purposes
- No gift tax consequences of funding

**Upon Grantor's Incapacity:**
- Successor trustee steps in automatically
- Avoids conservatorship proceeding
- Maintains privacy
- Manages assets per trust terms

**Upon Grantor's Death:**
- Property transfers to beneficiaries per trust terms
- No probate required
- Avoids delays, costs, publicity
- Successor trustee implements distribution plan

**Advantages:**
- Probate avoidance
- Incapacity planning (avoids conservatorship)
- Privacy (not public record)
- Efficient management of assets
- Clear succession plan
- Flexibility to change

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires asset funding (retitling)
- Trustee duties and accounting
- Ongoing trust management
- May create false sense of no probate (unfunded portions may probate)

### Irrevocable Trusts

**Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT)**
- Trustee (usually independent third party) owns life insurance
- Beneficiaries receive death proceeds tax-free
- Policy proceeds not included in taxable estate
- Allows large estates to pass tax-free
- Requires careful funding and documentation

**Qualified Terminable Interest Property (QTIP) Trust**
- Spouse receives income for life
- Remainder passes to designated beneficiaries (typically children)
- Defers estate tax until spouse's death
- Protects inheritance for children in blended family situations
- Marital deduction available for funding

**Irrevocable Charitable Remainder Trust**
- Donor receives income stream for life or term
- Remainder passes to charity at termination
- Income tax deduction for remainder value
- Estate tax exclusion of remainder
- Fixed or variable income

**Grantor Retained Annuity Trust (GRAT)**
- Grantor receives annuity for term of years
- Remainder passes to beneficiaries
- Freezes estate tax value at transfer
- Excellent for appreciating assets
- Requires term completion to achieve benefits

**Spousal Lifetime Access Trust (SLAT)**
- Grantor funds with estate/gift tax exemption
- Spouse has access to income/principal
- Remainder to children or other beneficiaries
- Uses gift tax exemption efficiently
- Creates leverage through growth

### Spendthrift Provisions
- Restriction on beneficiary's ability to transfer interest
- Creditor protection for beneficiary's creditors
- Preserves inheritance despite beneficiary's poor financial management
- Standard in modern trusts

## Incapacity Planning

### Problems Without Planning
- Family lacks authority to manage affairs
- Conservatorship proceeding required (public, costly, time-consuming)
- Family conflict over who should manage
- Disability benefits loss if improper management

### Durable Power of Attorney (Financial)
- Document authorizing agent to act on grantor's behalf
- Durable: Survives grantor's incapacity
- Grantor retains ultimate control (if competent)
- Can be limited to specific powers or general
- Immediate or springing (upon incapacity) effectiveness options

**Important Provisions:**
- Definition of incapacity (physician certification typically)
- Agent duties and restrictions
- Compensation for agent
- Agent succession if first unable/unwilling
- Limitations on agent authority

### Healthcare Power of Attorney and Advanced Directive
- Designate agent for healthcare decisions
- Authorizes agent to make medical decisions if grantor unable
- Living will provisions stating end-of-life wishes
- HIPAA authorization for medical information access
- Critical for serious illness scenarios

## Tax Planning in Estate Planning

### Federal Estate Tax
- Applies to estates exceeding lifetime exemption ($13.61 million in 2024, subject to phase-out)
- Tax rate: 40% on excess over exemption
- Unified with gift tax (lifetime exemption)
- Marital deduction for spouse (unlimited, with conditions)
- Charitable deduction for charitable gifts

### Gift Tax
- Tax on transfers during life
- Annual exclusion ($18,000 per recipient in 2024, non-taxable gifts)
- Lifetime exemption (same as estate tax)
- Gifts to spouse (unlimited, if citizen spouse)
- Tax returns (Form 709) may be required

### Income Tax in Estates and Trusts
- Complex income tax rules for estate/trust income
- Beneficiaries taxed on distributions
- Trust taxed on retained income
- Income in respect of decedent (IRD)
- Charitable deduction opportunities

### Step-Up in Basis
- Assets receive step-up in cost basis at death
- Heirs' income tax basis = fair market value at death (not cost basis)
- Eliminates income tax on appreciation during decedent's life
- Example: Stock purchased for $100,000, worth $250,000 at death → heir's basis $250,000

## Family Communication About Wealth

### Benefits of Communication
- Reduces conflict upon death
- Gives beneficiaries time to prepare
- Clarifies values and intentions
- Allows direction on wealth use
- Addresses questions while grantor available

### Communication Challenges
- Sensitive topic (death, money)
- Potential conflict between heirs
- Fairness perceptions
- Different values among family members
- Privacy concerns

### Communication Strategies
- Family meeting with advisor present
- Separate conversations with each adult child
- Letter of instruction regarding values and intentions
- Involvement of professional advisor (neutral third party)
- Begin early, update periodically

## Common Estate Planning Mistakes

1. **No Estate Plan or Outdated Plan**
   - Intestacy laws control, contrary to wishes
   - Probate costs and delays
   - Family conflict

2. **Failure to Fund Trusts**
   - Assets outside trust still probate
   - Trust remains unfunded/incomplete
   - Defeats probate avoidance objectives

3. **Incorrect Beneficiary Designations**
   - Name beneficiaries (spouse, exes, minors) incorrectly
   - Conflicts with will/trust
   - Unintended tax consequences

4. **Failure to Plan for Incapacity**
   - No authority for family to manage affairs
   - Expensive conservatorship if needed
   - Family conflict

5. **Unrealistic Guardianship Decisions**
   - Designated guardians unwilling or unable
   - No backup guardians
   - Children in crisis without designated plan

6. **Estate Concentration in One Asset**
   - Illiquid asset (business, real estate) must be sold to pay taxes
   - Liquidity planning insufficient

7. **Failure to Update Plan**
   - Life changes: Marriage, divorce, children, grandchildren
   - Tax law changes
   - Asset value changes
   - Location changes affecting jurisdiction

## Chapter Summary

Estate planning is essential for orderly transfer of property upon death and planning for incapacity. Revocable living trusts avoid probate while maintaining grantor control. Irrevocable trusts serve specialized purposes (tax reduction, asset protection, charitable planning). Powers of attorney for financial and healthcare matters ensure incapacity management. Tax planning integrates federal estate/gift taxation with income tax optimization. Family communication and regular plan updates ensure documents remain aligned with wishes and current law. Professional coordination among financial planner, attorney, tax advisor, and insurance advisor ensures comprehensive planning.
