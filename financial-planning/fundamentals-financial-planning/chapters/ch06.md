# Chapter 6. Case Analysis: The Burke Case Study

## Case Study Introduction

Case studies illustrate how the financial planning process applies to real-world situations. The Burke case demonstrates comprehensive planning for a middle-income family facing typical life events and planning challenges.

## The Burke Family Profile

**Demographics**
- Married couple (John and Mary Burke)
- Established family with children
- Mid-career earning capacity
- Moderate net worth
- Multiple concurrent financial goals

**Financial Situation Summary**
- Combined household income: Moderate to above-average range
- Existing investments and retirement accounts
- Primary residence with mortgage
- Life and health insurance coverage (existing but requires review)
- Estate planning documents (outdated, need revision)
- No comprehensive written financial plan to date

## The Planning Process Applied

### Step 1: Gathering Client Information

**Internal Data Collection**
- Family composition and ages
- Employment stability and benefits
- Income and expense history
- Asset and liability inventory
- Insurance policies and coverage
- Estate planning documents
- Education level and financial sophistication

**Qualitative Information Gathered**
- Retirement lifestyle vision and timing
- Children's education funding priorities
- Risk tolerance assessment
- Values regarding spending, saving, legacy
- Attitudes about debt and financial independence
- Career aspirations and expected income changes

**External Data Relevant to Burkes**
- Current interest rate environment
- Historical and expected investment returns
- Inflation forecasts
- Education cost trends
- Healthcare cost inflation
- Social Security benefit estimates
- Tax law and rates

### Step 2 & 3: Goal Identification and Analysis

**Primary Goals Identified**

1. **Retirement Security**
   - Desired retirement age (typically 10-15 years away in mid-career)
   - Lifestyle expectations in retirement
   - Capital needs calculation
   - Income sources (Social Security, pension if available, investments)

2. **Children's Education Funding**
   - College education for children
   - Public vs. private institution considerations
   - Timeline for education expenses
   - Available funding vehicles (529 plans, ESAs, taxable accounts)

3. **Debt Management**
   - Mortgage payoff strategy and timeline
   - Other debt elimination
   - Interest rate environment impact

4. **Risk Management**
   - Life insurance adequacy for income replacement
   - Disability insurance (especially primary earner)
   - Health insurance coordination and gaps
   - Liability protection and umbrella coverage

5. **Estate Planning**
   - Will updates for current family situation
   - Guardianship designations for minor children
   - Powers of attorney (financial and healthcare)
   - Trust planning considerations

### Step 4: Developing Recommendations

**Retirement Planning Analysis**
- Defined benefit and defined contribution plans available
- Social Security optimization (claiming age, spousal benefits)
- Needed savings rate to achieve retirement goal
- Investment allocation for time horizon
- Withdrawal strategy in retirement

**Education Funding Strategy**
- 529 plan contribution strategy (tax advantages, estate planning benefits)
- Allocation between parent and student/family responsibility
- Comparison to other funding sources
- Coordination with financial aid considerations (often detrimental)

**Debt Reduction Plan**
- Prioritization (credit cards first, then mortgage consideration)
- Accelerated payoff scenarios vs. normal amortization
- Impact on cash flow and other goals
- Tax considerations (mortgage interest, student loans)

**Insurance Review and Recommendations**
- Life insurance needs calculation
- Disability insurance gap analysis
- Health insurance coordination
- Additional umbrella liability coverage
- Beneficiary designation review

**Estate Plan Updates**
- Will for minor children guardianship
- Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and insurance
- Powers of attorney for incapacity planning
- Possible trust structures for protection and efficiency

## Analysis and Recommendations Presentation

### Communicating Findings

**Summary of Current Position**
- Strengths: Stable income, assets accumulated, insurance in place
- Weaknesses: Not funding education adequately, insufficient retirement savings trajectory, outdated estate documents

**Alternative Approaches Considered**
- Option 1: Aggressive debt payoff vs. investment focus
- Option 2: Tax-deferred education savings vs. taxable funding
- Option 3: Early retirement vs. phased retirement approach
- Presentation of trade-offs and client choice

**Specific Recommendations**
Prioritized by impact and urgency:

1. **Immediate Actions**
   - Update wills and designate guardians
   - Review and increase life insurance if needed
   - Establish education funding plan with 529 contributions

2. **Short-Term (1-2 years)**
   - Increase 401(k) contributions
   - Eliminate high-interest credit card debt
   - Review and adjust budget to support goals

3. **Intermediate (2-5 years)**
   - Consistent investment program toward retirement
   - Education funding increases as child approaches college
   - Consider home equity management (refi, payoff strategy)

4. **Long-Term (5+ years)**
   - Maintain investment discipline
   - Adjust allocation as retirement approaches
   - Monitor and update plan annually

## Implementation Phase

**Responsibility Assignment**
- Which recommendations does advisor implement
- Which does client handle directly
- Roles of accountant, attorney, insurance agent, investment manager

**Action Items Timeline**
- Specific steps with responsible parties and due dates
- Contingencies if life changes occur
- Communication protocol for updates

**Cost and Benefit Analysis**
- Implementation costs (advisory fees, product costs)
- Timeline for benefits realization
- ROI for each recommendation area

## Monitoring and Adjustment

### Annual Review Process
- Update personal financial statements
- Reassess goals for continued relevance
- Review progress toward objectives
- Market and economic condition changes
- Tax law changes impact

### Trigger Events for Plan Revision
- Significant income changes (raise, job change, retirement)
- Family changes (birth, death, marriage, divorce)
- Health events affecting ability to work
- Market events creating rebalancing need
- Goal achievement or revision

## Key Lessons from Case Application

### Planning Process Effectiveness
- Comprehensive analysis prevents overlooking important areas
- Written recommendations create accountability
- Regular monitoring catches needed adjustments early
- Case study demonstrates value of professional planning

### Common Planning Challenges
- Competing goals requiring prioritization
- Trade-offs between retirement saving and education funding
- Behavioral tendencies (overconfidence, underinsurance, procrastination)
- Changing circumstances requiring flexibility

### Implementation Realities
- Ideal plan requires behavior change and discipline
- Advisors help overcome psychological barriers
- Coordination across multiple professionals often necessary
- Ongoing monitoring more important than initial planning

## Chapter Summary

The Burke case study demonstrates how comprehensive financial planning addresses multiple concurrent goals through systematic analysis. The planning process identifies strengths to build upon and weaknesses to address, considers alternative approaches, and provides prioritized recommendations with clear implementation assignments. Regular monitoring and adjustment ensure the plan remains relevant as circumstances change. This case illustrates both the complexity of real-world financial planning and the significant value created through professional guidance.
