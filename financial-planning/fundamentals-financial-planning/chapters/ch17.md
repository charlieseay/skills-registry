# Chapter 17. Planning for Special Circumstances

## Introduction

While comprehensive financial planning addresses universal life goals (retirement, education, tax optimization, estate planning), certain life circumstances create unique planning challenges and opportunities. This chapter addresses specialized planning situations.

## Planning for Individuals with Special Needs

### Overview
Individuals with disabilities (physical, mental, developmental) require specialized financial planning that integrates public benefits, private resources, and long-term care planning.

### Types of Disabilities
- Developmental disabilities (Down syndrome, autism)
- Mental illness (schizophrenia, bipolar disorder)
- Physical disabilities (cerebral palsy, spinal cord injury)
- Cognitive impairments (dementia, Alzheimer's)
- Chronic illnesses (cystic fibrosis, sickle cell disease)

### Public Benefits Overview
- **SSI (Supplemental Security Income):** Needs-based assistance, income/asset limits
- **SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance):** Earned benefit, no income/asset limits
- **Medicaid:** State health insurance, income/asset limits
- **Medicare:** Federal health insurance (SSDI recipients)
- **Public Housing:** Subsidized housing, asset/income limits

### Asset and Income Limits
- SSI: Typically $2,000 individual, $3,000 couple asset limit
- Medicaid: Varies by state
- Excess assets can disqualify for needs-based benefits
- Income from gifts or settlements can create complications

### Planning Vehicles

**Special Needs Trust (SNT)**
- Trustee manages assets for disabled person's benefit
- Trust assets not countable for public benefits
- Does not impact SSI/Medicaid eligibility
- Trustee may supplement public benefits with private resources
- Can pay for education, therapy, recreational activities
- Cannot pay for food/shelter (would reduce SSI)

**ABLE Account (Achieving a Better Life Experience)**
- Tax-advantaged savings account for individuals with disabilities
- $18,000 annual contribution limit
- Accounts up to $235,000 before SSI suspension
- Over $235,000, SSI suspended (but Medicaid continues)
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals for qualified expenses
- Portable if account holder moves

**Third-Party Special Needs Trust**
- Funded by parent or grandparent (not the disabled person)
- Trustee discretion on distributions
- Supplements public benefits
- Survives beyond beneficiary's lifetime
- Primary planning tool for special needs

**Self-Settled SNT (First-Party Trust)**
- Funded with disabled person's assets (inheritance, settlement, lawsuit recovery)
- Subject to repayment requirement (state must be reimbursed for Medicaid)
- Limits trustee flexibility
- Protects Medicaid eligibility while providing supplements
- Less commonly used

### Coordination with Public Benefits
- Careful trust design essential to avoid unintended benefit loss
- Food and shelter payments from SNT reduce SSI
- Medical expenses can be paid from SNT (don't affect SSI)
- Recreation, education, therapy appropriate from SNT
- Ongoing benefits planning as circumstances change

### Letter of Intent
- Document describing disabled person's needs, preferences, capabilities
- Information for future guardian/trustee
- Describes daily routines, medical needs, living situation
- Communication method preferences
- Guardianship wishes
- Provides continuity when primary caregiver changes

## Planning for Divorce

### Pre-Divorce Financial Assessment
- Comprehensive net worth statement (both spouses)
- Income documentation (employment, business, investments)
- Asset valuation (real estate, business, investments)
- Debt inventory (mortgages, loans, credit cards)
- Insurance policies and beneficiaries
- Estate planning documents

### Financial Implications of Divorce

**Asset Division**
- Community property states: 50/50 division principle (absent agreement)
- Common law states: "Equitable" division (fair, not necessarily equal)
- Business valuation complex
- Retirement account division requires QDRO (Qualified Domestic Relations Order)
- Home and real estate often primary assets

**Spousal Support (Alimony)**
- Duration depends on marriage length and agreement
- Amount based on income differential and need
- Tax treatment post-2018: No deduction for payer, no income for recipient
- May be modified if circumstances change
- Structured as fixed term or indefinite

**Child Support**
- Determined by state guidelines based on income
- Continues until age of majority
- Can be modified if significant income change
- Not tax-deductible
- Additional expenses (education, health) may be shared

**Tax Implications**
- Filing status changes
- Dependency exemption decisions
- Child tax credits allocation
- Earned income tax credit eligibility
- Deduction coordination for home, investments

### Post-Divorce Planning

**Update Estate Plan**
- Revise will (ex-spouse may be heir otherwise)
- Update beneficiary designations (life insurance, retirement)
- Modify power of attorney and healthcare directives
- Address guardianship designations

**Adjust Financial Plan**
- Single income household
- New budget and expense priorities
- Insurance needs assessment
- Retirement plan modification
- Emergency fund establishment

**Psychological Transition**
- Advisors facilitate financial independence
- Education about self-directed finances
- Rebuilding financial confidence
- Managing financial stress during transition

## Planning for Job Loss or Career Change

### Financial Impact Assessment
- Income replacement (severance, unemployment benefits)
- Emergency fund adequacy
- Health insurance continuation (COBRA, ACA marketplace)
- Retirement account access and restrictions
- Mortgage and debt obligations

### Emergency Fund Adequacy
- 3-6 months of essential expenses minimum
- 9-12 months if uncertain employment (independent contractor, volatile industry)
- Unemployment benefits (typically 4-6 months duration)
- Severance packages (vary widely)
- Spousal income available

### Health Insurance Options
- COBRA continuation (18-36 months of prior employer coverage)
- ACA marketplace insurance (subsidies available based on income)
- Spouse's employer plan
- Professional association plans
- Individual policies (often limited in special needs situations)

### Retirement Account Considerations
- 401(k) access varies by termination terms
- IRA funds accessible (but with income tax and potential penalty)
- Rule of 55 allows 401(k) access at 55+ without penalty
- Roth conversion during low-income period opportunity
- Early distribution penalties and exceptions

### Career Change Planning
- Retraining and education costs
- Income reduction during transition
- Timeline for new career earnings
- Temporary reduction in savings rate
- Long-term income potential vs. current stability

### Psychological and Behavioral Dimensions
- Financial stress and anxiety common
- May lead to poor financial decision-making
- Advisor role as stabilizing influence
- Realistic assessment of options
- Action plan and timeline help restore sense of control

## Planning for Serious Illness and Terminal Condition

### Preparation and Acceptance
- Advance medical directives (if not already done)
- Healthcare power of attorney designation
- End-of-life preferences documentation
- Family communication about wishes
- Peace of mind from preparation

### Financial Organization
- Consolidate financial statements and account information
- Insurance policy locations and details
- Bills and debt inventory
- Beneficiary designations review
- Tax return files and documentation

### Income and Expense Planning
- Disability income (if available)
- Medical expense budgeting
- Insurance coverage for treatment
- Medicare/Medicaid coordination
- Family financial support planning

### Estate and Legacy Planning
- Will or trust updates
- Charitable giving considerations
- Legacy letters to family
- Video/audio recording of values/wishes
- Instructions for digital assets

### Family and Advisor Coordination
- Spouse education about finances
- Adult children involvement (appropriate level)
- Professional advisor coordination (attorney, accountant, insurance)
- Succession planning for decision-making
- Communication protocol during crisis

## Planning for Non-Traditional Households

### Domestic Partnerships and Civil Unions
- Legal recognition varies by state
- Tax consequences differ from marriage
- Inheritance and succession planning implications
- Healthcare decision-making authority
- Spousal benefits and protections

### Same-Sex Couples
- Prior to marriage legalization: Complex planning required
- Post-marriage: Tax benefits and spousal protections apply
- Estate planning to ensure recognition
- Adoption and parenting considerations
- Healthcare and medical decision-making

### Unmarried Cohabiting Couples
- No automatic legal recognition (vs. marriage)
- Separate property unless structured otherwise
- Limited inheritance rights (no spousal preference)
- Healthcare decision-making difficult without documentation
- Planning requires explicit agreements

### Multigenerational Households
- Aging parents and adult children living together
- Financial interdependence (shared expenses, support)
- Estate and guardianship planning complexity
- Income tax and dependent status implications
- Care planning and succession

## Planning for Financial Windfalls

### Types of Windfalls
- Large inheritance
- Lawsuit settlement
- Bonus or stock option exercise
- Business sale
- Lottery or contest winnings
- Sudden wealth from any source

### Psychological Challenges
- Decision paralysis (overwhelmed by options)
- Fear of making wrong choice
- Pressure from family and friends
- Sudden lifestyle inflation
- Loss of motivation or focus

### Prudent Approach to Windfall Management

**Step 1: Delay Major Decisions**
- Don't immediately spend or invest
- Take 6-12 months before major commitment
- Resist pressure for immediate decisions
- Allow emotions to settle

**Step 2: Obtain Professional Guidance**
- Accountant for tax implications
- Attorney for legal structures
- Financial planner for investment/allocation
- Insurance advisor for protection
- Multidisciplinary team coordination

**Step 3: Create Plan**
- Allocate to goals (emergency fund, debt, savings, investments)
- Prioritize financial security (emergency fund, insurance)
- Address debt (especially high-interest)
- Determine appropriate spending increase
- Charitable giving if desired

**Step 4: Implement Systematically**
- Don't deploy all at once
- Diversification reduces concentration risk
- Tax-efficient positioning
- Regular review and adjustment
- Discipline to maintain plan

### Windfall Planning by Type

**Inheritance**
- Probate and estate tax issues
- Inherited IRA consideration (required distributions)
- Stepped-up basis for tax efficiency
- Family relationship maintenance
- Charitable intentions integration

**Business Sale**
- Capital gains planning and tax management
- Installment sale vs. lump sum
- Post-sale financial planning
- Diversification from concentrated position
- Retirement planning update

**Lottery/Winnings**
- Lump sum vs. annuity choice (tax and NPV considerations)
- Immediate security focus
- Family protection from scams
- Privacy considerations
- Professional guidance essential

## Chapter Summary

Special circumstances including disabilities, divorce, job loss, serious illness, non-traditional households, and financial windfalls require customized financial planning adapted to unique situations. Planning for special needs individuals integrates public benefits, trusts, and family support. Divorce planning addresses asset division, spousal support, and family financial reorganization. Job loss planning focuses on emergency funds, income replacement, and benefit continuity. Serious illness planning ensures documentation of wishes and family financial organization. Non-traditional households require explicit legal structures to ensure recognition and protection. Windfall planning emphasizes systematic, deliberate implementation over rushed decision-making. Professional advisors help navigate complexity, manage psychological factors, and implement coordinated solutions.
