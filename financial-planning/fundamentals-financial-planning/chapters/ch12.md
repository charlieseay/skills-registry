# Chapter 12. Income Tax Planning

## Tax Planning Fundamentals

### Tax Avoidance vs. Tax Evasion

**Tax Planning (Legal)**
- Organizing affairs to minimize tax burden
- Using legal provisions to reduce taxes
- Taking advantage of available deductions and credits
- Timing income and expenses strategically
- Choosing advantageous entity structures

**Tax Evasion (Illegal)**
- Intentionally failing to report income
- Claiming false deductions
- Concealing transactions from tax authorities
- Violating tax laws

Professional financial planners engage in tax planning, never tax evasion.

### Tax Planning Goals

1. **Minimize Current Tax Burden**
   - Maximize deductions and credits
   - Timing of income/expenses
   - Use of tax-deferred accounts

2. **Manage Tax-Deferred Growth**
   - Retirement accounts, life insurance
   - Deferral of capital gains
   - Deferred compensation plans

3. **Optimize Long-Term After-Tax Returns**
   - Lifetime tax perspective, not single year
   - Consideration of heirs' tax situations
   - Intergenerational tax planning

## Income Classification and Rates

### Ordinary Income
- Taxed at marginal tax rates (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37% in 2023)
- Includes wages, interest, ordinary business income, short-term capital gains
- Highest tax burden among income types

### Capital Gains Income
- Long-term capital gains: 0%, 15%, 20% rates (preferential)
- Short-term capital gains: Taxed as ordinary income
- Holding period determination (>1 year = long-term)
- Dividends eligible for preferential rates

### Tax-Exempt Income
- Municipal bond interest
- Life insurance proceeds (upon death)
- Certain government benefits
- No federal tax liability

### Tax-Deferred Income
- Retirement account contributions (traditional IRAs, 401(k)s)
- Growth within tax-deferred accounts
- Taxed upon withdrawal
- Advantages: Deferral benefit, larger account growth

## Deductions and Credits

### Standard vs. Itemized Deductions

**Itemized Deductions** (if exceeding standard deduction)
- Mortgage interest (limited to $750,000 acquisition debt)
- State and local taxes (SALT) capped at $10,000
- Charitable contributions (if documenting)
- Medical expenses (exceeding 7.5% AGI threshold)

**Standard Deduction** (2023 amounts, adjusted annually)
- Single: $13,850
- Married filing jointly: $27,700
- Head of household: $20,800
- Dependent: $1,700+ (earned income or $5,150)

**Strategy**: Calculate both and use higher amount.

### Credits (Direct Reduction of Tax)

**Child and Dependent Credits**
- Child Tax Credit: $2,000 per qualifying child
- Credit for Other Dependents: $500
- Income limitations (phaseout begins $400,000 MFJ)

**Education Credits**
- American Opportunity Credit: $2,500 per student
- Lifetime Learning Credit: $2,000 per return
- Coordinated with 529 distributions

**Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)**
- Refundable credit for low-income working families
- Three-tiered benefit (one, two, three+ children)
- Income limitations

**Others**
- Retirement savings credit (Saver's Credit)
- Adoption credit
- Residential energy credits
- Other targeted credits

## Retirement Account Tax Planning

### Traditional vs. Roth Contributions

**Traditional IRA/401(k)**
- Contributions reduce taxable income (deductible)
- Tax-deferred growth
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) at age 73
- Withdrawals taxed as ordinary income

**Roth IRA/Roth 401(k)**
- Contributions not tax-deductible
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals
- No Required Minimum Distributions
- Tax-free heirs' withdrawals (for inherited Roth)

**Decision Framework**
- Current vs. expected future tax rates
- Roth beneficial if expecting higher rates later
- Traditional beneficial if in high tax bracket now
- Income limits for Roth contributions

### Contribution Strategies

**401(k) Maximization**
- Employee contribution: $22,500 (2023)
- Catch-up (age 50+): Additional $7,500
- Employer match: Maximize if available
- Coordination with spouse if both working

**IRA Contributions**
- Traditional or Roth: $6,500 annual (2023)
- Catch-up (age 50+): Additional $1,000
- Spousal IRA if one spouse has no income
- Limit total traditional/Roth contributions

**Solo Entrepreneur Options**
- Solo 401(k): Up to $66,000 (2023)
- SEP-IRA: Up to 25% of net self-employment income
- Defined Benefit Plan: Larger contributions for older business owners
- SIMPLE IRA: For small employers

## Investment Tax Planning

### Asset Location Strategy

**Tax-Deferred Accounts (401k, Traditional IRA)**
- Best for high-turnover investments
- Tax-inefficient investments (bonds, REITs)
- No tax drag on growth

**Taxable Accounts**
- Tax-efficient investments (stocks with low turnover)
- Municipal bonds (tax-exempt interest)
- Low-dividend growth stocks

**Tax-Loss Harvesting**
- Sell losing positions to offset gains
- Immediate loss recognition
- Wash-sale rule: Cannot repurchase substantially identical security within 30 days
- Can use for future year gains if current year losses exceed gains

### Dividend and Capital Gains Management

**Qualified Dividends (0%, 15%, 20% rates)**
- Held >60 days around ex-dividend date
- From U.S. corporations or qualified foreign corporations
- REITs and municipal bond dividends do not qualify

**Strategic Realization**
- Harvest losses to offset gains in winning positions
- Defer gains until higher income years (retirement)
- Coordinate with charitable donations
- Track holding periods carefully

## Estimated Tax Payments

### Quarterly Requirements
Self-employed and high-income individuals must make:
- Estimated tax payments quarterly (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15)
- Based on expected annual tax liability
- Penalty if underpaying (safe harbor: 90% current year or 100% prior year)

### Adjustments
- Increase if expecting higher income
- Decrease if expecting lower income
- Withholding adjustment if W-2 income changes
- Timely payments prevent penalties

## Business Owner Tax Planning

### Entity Selection

**Sole Proprietorship**
- No separate tax entity
- Income taxed at personal rates
- Self-employment tax on all net income (15.3%)
- Unlimited personal liability

**Partnership or LLC (pass-through)**
- Business profits "pass through" to owner tax returns
- Taxed at individual rates
- Potential self-employment tax savings through reasonable salary
- Choice of entity vs. partnership taxation

**S-Corporation**
- Election to be taxed as corporation
- Significant self-employment tax savings (officer salary reduces SE tax)
- Requires payroll system (quarterly filings)
- State filing and compliance requirements

**C-Corporation**
- Double taxation (corporate and shareholder level)
- Rarely advisable unless specific circumstances
- May use for retained earnings if profits reinvested

### Business Deductions

**Commonly Deductible Expenses**
- Office supplies and equipment
- Professional services (accounting, legal, consulting)
- Advertising and marketing
- Vehicle expenses (actual or standard mileage)
- Home office deduction (careful documentation)
- Retirement plan contributions
- Health insurance (self-employed)

**Non-Deductible Personal Expenses**
- Personal travel not related to business
- Country club and social memberships
- Commuting
- Meals and entertainment (limited to 50%)
- Personal vehicle expenses

## Tax Coordination Across Life Events

### Marriage
- Filing status change (joint vs. separate)
- Income timing coordination
- Deduction optimization (mortgage interest, SALT)
- Potential for reduced overall tax burden

### Divorce
- Alimony deductibility changes (post-2018 law)
- Dependency exemption coordination
- Asset division tax consequences
- Business valuation implications

### Business Sale
- Capital gains planning
- Installment sale treatment (spreads gains over years)
- Contingent compensation arrangements
- Tax-loss planning in subsequent years

### Inheritance
- Step-up in basis for inherited assets
- Timing of distributions
- Income in respect of decedent (IRD)
- Charitable giving integration

## Chapter Summary

Income tax planning integrates throughout financial planning by considering after-tax impact of all recommendations. Tax planning includes maximizing retirement contributions and using tax-advantaged accounts, strategic placement of investments in tax-efficient locations, harvesting capital losses to offset gains, and timing income and expenses to minimize lifetime tax burden. Business owners benefit from careful entity selection and expense management. Tax planning requires annual review as tax law changes and individual circumstances evolve.
