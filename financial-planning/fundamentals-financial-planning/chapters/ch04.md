# Chapter 4. Personal Financial Statements: Preparation and Analysis

## The Balance Sheet (Net Worth Statement)

The balance sheet shows a snapshot of financial position at a specific point in time, listing all assets, liabilities, and net worth.

### Basic Equation
**Assets - Liabilities = Net Worth (Equity)**

### Classification of Assets

**Liquid Assets**
- Cash and cash equivalents
- Checking and savings accounts
- Money market funds
- Certificates of deposit (CDs)
- Purpose: Emergency reserves, immediate obligations
- Guideline: 3-6 months of living expenses recommended

**Investment Assets**
- Stocks and mutual funds
- Bonds and bond funds
- Real estate investment trusts (REITs)
- Precious metals
- Purpose: Long-term growth and income
- Consideration: Diversification, time horizon, risk tolerance

**Retirement Accounts**
- Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs)
- 401(k) and 403(b) plans
- SEP-IRAs and SIMPLE IRAs
- Employer pension plans
- Tax treatment: Pre-tax contributions, tax-deferred growth (with some exceptions)
- Withdrawal restrictions and penalties

**Personal Use Assets**
- Primary residence
- Automobiles
- Furniture and household goods
- Art, jewelry, collectibles
- Valuation: Fair market value, not insurance value
- Liquidation: May require time and may not achieve estimated value
- Income production: Generally none (though home may be rental property)

**Business Interests**
- Sole proprietorship equity
- Partnership interests
- Closely held business stock
- Valuation: Complex, professional appraisal often required
- Liquidity: Often very low
- Tax considerations: Significant

### Classification of Liabilities

**Short-Term Liabilities**
- Credit card balances
- Accounts payable
- Current installment of long-term debt
- Accrued expenses and taxes due
- Timeline: Due within 12 months

**Long-Term Liabilities**
- Mortgage debt
- Auto loans
- Student loans
- Business loans
- Timeline: Due beyond 12 months
- Interest rates: May be fixed or variable

**Contingent Liabilities**
- Potential legal judgments
- Guarantee of another's debt
- Pending tax disputes
- Not on standard balance sheet but important to disclose

### Net Worth Analysis

**Net Worth Growth Over Time**
- Track changes year to year
- Identify sources of growth (income, investment gains, debt paydown)
- Compare to goals and expectations
- Adjust planning if growth inadequate

**Asset Mix Analysis**
- Percentage in liquid, investment, retirement, and personal use assets
- Appropriate balance depends on life stage and goals
- Too much tied up in personal residence reduces flexibility
- Insufficient liquid assets creates emergency vulnerability

**Liability Management**
- Total debt relative to income
- Debt service ratio (monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income)
- Ideal: Less than 30-35% for sustainable debt
- High ratios indicate cash flow stress and limited flexibility

## The Income Statement (Cash Flow Statement)

The income statement summarizes income and expenses over a period (typically monthly or annually) and shows cash flow available after expenses.

### Income Categories

**Earned Income**
- Salary and wages
- Self-employment income
- Bonuses and commissions
- Side business income
- Timing: Regular, predictable for employment; variable for self-employment

**Investment Income**
- Dividends
- Interest
- Capital gains
- Rental income (net of expenses)
- Timing: May be quarterly, annual, or irregular
- Tax treatment: Often taxed at different rates than earned income

**Other Income**
- Social Security benefits
- Pension distributions
- Alimony or child support received
- Inheritance or gifts
- Tax-free income (municipal bonds, life insurance proceeds, etc.)

**Gross Income Calculation**
Sum all income sources before tax and expenses to understand total earning capacity.

### Expense Categories

**Housing Expenses**
- Mortgage or rent
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Utilities
- Maintenance and repairs
- Total as % of gross income: Generally should not exceed 28%

**Debt Service**
- Credit card payments
- Auto loan payments
- Student loan payments
- Personal loan payments
- Determines financial flexibility and borrowing capacity

**Transportation**
- Auto insurance
- Fuel
- Maintenance and repairs
- Public transportation
- Registration and tags
- As % of gross income: 15-20% is common

**Food and Groceries**
- Household food purchases
- Dining out and restaurants
- Often largest discretionary category
- Varies significantly by family size and lifestyle

**Insurance**
- Health insurance premiums
- Life insurance
- Disability insurance
- Long-term care insurance
- Umbrella liability coverage

**Taxes**
- Federal income tax (or estimated quarterly payments)
- State income tax
- Local taxes
- Self-employment tax
- Property tax (if not included in mortgage)

**Personal and Discretionary**
- Clothing and personal care
- Entertainment and recreation
- Subscriptions and memberships
- Travel and vacations
- Gifts and charitable contributions
- Hobbies

**Children and Education**
- Childcare and daycare
- School tuition and fees
- Tutoring and lessons
- College and advanced education

**Other**
- Medical and dental (after insurance)
- Pet care
- Phone and internet
- Professional services

### Cash Flow Analysis

**Surplus or Deficit**
- Income exceeding expenses = surplus (money available for savings/goals)
- Expenses exceeding income = deficit (unsustainable, requires borrowing or drawing down savings)

**Discretionary vs. Non-Discretionary Spending**
- Non-discretionary (housing, utilities, insurance): Cannot easily reduce
- Discretionary (entertainment, dining, gifts): Can adjust based on priorities
- Understanding distinction helps identify where adjustments are possible

**Emergency Expense Planning**
- Budget should include category for emergency reserves
- Unexpected car repairs, medical costs, home repairs
- Without emergency fund, these trigger debt accumulation

**Tax Planning Opportunity**
- Estimated tax payments can be adjusted
- Withholding on W-4 can be modified
- Timing of income and deductions affects annual tax burden

## Comprehensive Financial Analysis Ratios

### Liquidity Ratios

**Current Ratio = Liquid Assets ÷ Annual Living Expenses**
- Measures months of expenses covered by liquid assets
- Rule of thumb: 0.5 to 1.0 (6-12 months expenses)
- Too low: Vulnerability to emergency without debt
- Too high: Excess cash earning minimal returns

**Emergency Fund Adequacy**
- Minimum: 3 months of essential expenses
- Better: 6 months for variable income or job uncertainty
- Calculation: Identify essential monthly expenses, multiply by desired months

### Debt Ratios

**Debt-to-Income Ratio = Total Monthly Debt Payments ÷ Gross Monthly Income**
- Measures debt burden relative to earning capacity
- Maximum sustainable: 35-40% for total debt, 28% for housing alone
- Higher ratios limit borrowing capacity and financial flexibility
- Lower ratios indicate better financial position

**Debt-to-Asset Ratio = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets**
- Shows percentage of assets financed by debt
- Higher ratio indicates less equity ownership
- Too high: More vulnerable to market downturns
- Target: Depends on life stage (younger can be higher due to mortgage)

### Savings Rate

**Savings Rate = Annual Savings ÷ Gross Annual Income**
- Measures portion of income directed to future goals
- Industry benchmark: 10-20% for adequate retirement savings
- Essential for building wealth and achieving financial independence
- Varies by life stage (higher in earning years, lower in early career)

### Asset Allocation

**Percentage of assets in each category**
- Liquid vs. invested vs. personal use
- Diversification within investment assets
- Appropriate for time horizon and goals
- Reviewed and adjusted as circumstances change

## Using Financial Statements for Planning

### Baseline Assessment
Financial statements establish starting point for planning:
- What's working well in current situation
- Where vulnerabilities exist
- How close client is to goal achievement
- What changes are needed

### Goal Feasibility
Comparing current savings rate, net worth growth trajectory, and expenses to goals:
- Determine if current plan will achieve goals
- Identify where adjustments are necessary
- Quantify impact of choices and trade-offs

### Monitoring Progress
Annual preparation and comparison of statements shows:
- Whether net worth is growing as projected
- Changes in income and expenses
- Movement toward financial goals
- Need for plan adjustments

### Identifying Opportunities
Analysis reveals:
- Expense reductions that improve cash flow
- Tax-saving opportunities
- Debt reduction priorities
- Asset reallocation for better returns

## Chapter Summary

Personal financial statements form the foundation of financial planning by showing current position (balance sheet), annual cash flow (income statement), and using financial ratios to analyze adequacy and identify planning opportunities. Understanding personal balance sheets—classifying assets and liabilities appropriately and calculating net worth—provides a clear picture of financial position. Cash flow analysis identifies available resources for goal funding and reveals where adjustments may be necessary. Financial ratios measure liquidity, debt burden, savings rate, and diversification. Regular preparation and analysis of financial statements tracks progress toward goals and identifies needed adjustments in the planning process.
