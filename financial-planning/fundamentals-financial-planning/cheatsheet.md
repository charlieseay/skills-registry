# Financial Planning Quick Reference Cheatsheet

## The 7-Step Financial Planning Process (CFP Board)

| Step | Purpose | Key Activities |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1. Understand Circumstances | Establish foundation | Gather data, understand goals, assess values |
| 2. Identify Goals | Define objectives | Prioritize, resolve conflicts, clarify timeline |
| 3. Analyze Situation | Evaluate options | Cash flow, ratios, gap analysis |
| 4. Develop Recommendations | Determine strategy | Create integrated plan, prioritize solutions |
| 5. Present Plan | Get agreement | Clear communication, discuss trade-offs |
| 6. Implement | Execute decisions | Coordinate, assign responsibility, fund |
| 7. Monitor & Update | Track progress | Annual review, adjust as needed |

## Financial Statement Quick Analysis

### Balance Sheet (Net Worth)

**Key Ratios:**
- **Current Ratio** = Liquid Assets ÷ Annual Expenses (Target: 0.5-1.0 = 6-12 months)
- **Debt-to-Income** = Total Debt Payments ÷ Gross Income (Target: <35-40%)
- **Debt-to-Asset** = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets (Varies by life stage)

**Asset Mix (Suggested):**
- Early Career: 90% investments, 5% liquid, 5% personal use
- Building Years: 70% investments, 10% liquid, 20% personal use
- Pre-Retirement: 50% investments, 15% liquid, 35% personal use
- Retirement: 30% investments, 20% liquid, 50% personal use

### Income Statement (Cash Flow)

**Key Calculation:**
- Gross Income - All Expenses = **Surplus or Deficit**
- **Savings Rate** = Annual Savings ÷ Gross Income (Target: 10-20%)

**Expense Allocation (Recommended):**
- Housing: 28% of income max
- Transportation: 15-20% of income
- Food: 10-15% of income
- Insurance: 10-15% of income
- Debt Service: <35% of income
- Taxes: Federal, state, local rates vary
- Discretionary: Remaining after essential

- 0% rate: Single $0-$47,025; MFJ $0-$94,050

- SALT (state/local): Capped at $10,000 total

## Debt Prioritization Strategy

**Quick Rule - Pay Debts in This Order:**
1. **Immediate Priority:**
   - Credit cards (highest rate, highest tax inefficiency)
   - Payday loans
   - Any debt in collection or default

2. **Secondary Priority:**
   - Auto loans (collateral risk)
   - Personal loans
   - Medical debt

3. **Lower Priority (after above cleared):**
   - Mortgage (low rate, tax deductible)
   - Student loans (lowest rate, forgiveness options)

**Calculation: Savings vs. Debt Payoff**
- If debt rate < expected investment return: Invest
- If debt rate > expected return: Pay off debt
- Consider psychological aspect (debt reduction confidence)

## Insurance Needs Calculation

### Life Insurance
**Formula:** (Income Replacement × Years to Retirement) + Debts + Final Expenses - Existing Assets

**Minimum Replacement (Conservative):** 5-10x annual income

**Education Fund Addition:** $100,000-$500,000 per child (depends on goals)

### Disability Insurance
**Typical Need:** 60-70% of income replacement, 90-day waiting period

**Coverage Duration:** To age 65 (recommended for career-long protection)

### Emergency Fund
**Months of Expenses to Hold:**
- Stable employment, single income: 3-6 months
- Variable income, dual earners: 6-9 months
- Self-employed, job uncertainty: 9-12 months

### Long-Term Care Insurance
**Consider If:**
- Assets <$2 million (self-insurance not viable)
- Family history of dementia/Alzheimer's
- Age 60-75 (outside this window: expensive or unavailable)
- Income sufficient to afford premiums (5-7% of income red flag)

## Investment Allocation by Life Stage

### Age 20-35 (Accumulation)
**Allocation:** 90% stocks / 10% bonds
**Strategy:** Maximum risk capacity, dollar-cost average contributions, ignore volatility

### Age 35-50 (Building)
**Allocation:** 70-80% stocks / 20-30% bonds
**Strategy:** Accelerate retirement savings, diversify beyond primary asset class

### Age 50-60 (Pre-Retirement)
**Allocation:** 60-70% stocks / 30-40% bonds
**Strategy:** Increase contributions with catch-up, reduce volatility, plan distribution strategy

### Age 60-65 (Late Accumulation)
**Allocation:** 50-60% stocks / 40-50% bonds
**Strategy:** Risk reduction, plan Social Security claiming, retirement income plan

### Age 65+ (Distribution)
**Allocation:** 40-50% stocks / 50-60% bonds
**Strategy:** Income generation, manage sequence of returns risk, Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

## Estate Planning Checklist

**For Every Adult:**
- [ ] Will or revocable living trust
- [ ] Healthcare power of attorney
- [ ] Financial power of attorney
- [ ] Beneficiary designations reviewed/updated
- [ ] Letter of intent (instructions for heirs)

**For Married with Children:**
- [ ] Spouse named executor/successor trustee
- [ ] Guardians designated for minors
- [ ] Spouse/children beneficiaries appropriate
- [ ] Life insurance proceeds to trust if under age
- [ ] Education funding plan (529, UGMA, trust)

**For High Net Worth (>$5 million):**
- [ ] Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (remove from taxable estate)
- [ ] Gifting strategy (use annual exclusion)
- [ ] Dynasty trust or generation-skipping planning
- [ ] Charitable remainder trust (if charitable interested)
- [ ] Revocable trust funded with all assets (probate avoidance)

**For Business Owners:**
- [ ] Buy-sell agreement in place
- [ ] Succession plan documented
- [ ] Business valuation current
- [ ] Key person insurance funding
- [ ] Entity structure reviewed (tax efficiency)

## Financial Planning Red Flags

**Immediate Action Needed:**
- ⚠️ No emergency fund with income-dependent family
- ⚠️ Debt exceeding 40% of income
- ⚠️ No life insurance with dependents
- ⚠️ No disability insurance for primary earner
- ⚠️ Savings rate <3% of income for non-retired
- ⚠️ No estate plan (adults with minor children)

**Urgent Attention Required:**
- 🚩 Single asset >50% of net worth (concentration)
- 🚩 Educational funding not started with <5 years to college
- 🚩 Inadequate emergency fund for job uncertainty
- 🚩 Retiring with <75% capital needs secured
- 🚩 No long-term care planning with high risk

## Financial Planning Communication Tips

**For Visual Learners:** Use charts, graphs, illustrations, diagrams
**For Verbal Learners:** Use clear explanations, written summaries, detailed discussion
**For Analytical Clients:** Provide detailed reports, explain assumptions, show calculations
**For Intuitive Clients:** Start with big picture, high-level summary, key insights

**In All Cases:**
- Use plain English (avoid jargon or explain terms)
- Connect recommendations to stated goals
- Explain both benefits and risks
- Discuss trade-offs and alternatives
- Confirm understanding with follow-up questions
- Provide written summary of recommendations

## Behavioral Finance Quick Reference

**Common Biases Affecting Financial Decisions:**
- **Overconfidence:** Thinks they know more than they do
- **Loss Aversion:** Fears losses more than appreciates gains
- **Recency Bias:** Overweights recent events
- **Anchoring:** Fixates on initial price point
- **Herding:** Follows crowd into popular investments
- **Confirmation Bias:** Seeks information confirming beliefs
- **Mental Accounting:** Treats different money pools separately

**Advisor Role:** Recognize bias in client (and self), help overcome through data/perspective, implement commitment devices (auto-invest, rebalancing rules)
